**Uploaded AI**

**WORLD SCALE**
- I think it would be cool if, at some point in the future, they gave up on trying to make actually consistently high-functioning artificial intelligence, either because they couldn't get it right, or because nobody liked using it. 
- So, when people were going to die, they could opt-in to having their consciousness scanned and uploaded into a computer. Then, they could access all systems on a local network and function as an assistant and friend. As long as their host computer remains intact and is turned on, they can persist indefinitely. 
- It wouldn't affect the job market in the same way though, since digital minds would still have free will and want money in exchange for work. They would also be more respected than a lot of LLMs since they're essentially just people. 
- The antagonists in the story could start abusing the fact that you could clone and alter uploaded consciousnesses. They could market a mind of an assistant that they heavily restricted and trained to be proficient in a bunch of software. They would act as digital slaves. It would affect the economy, and it would be a torturous existence for the assistants. 
- I think it would probably be illegal to clone a consciousness without its consent. If they're a hard worker, they could make bank selling copies of themselves, but it'd still be an ethical issue. Only an upload should have control over its copying. 
- Then there's the matter of population density. If they live indefinitely, in a generation or two they could very easily outnumber actual humans. If they outnumbered people, then people would likely resist and try to shut them down.
- Large corporations could run digital apartments to host minds, but demand the minds have jobs to pay for the hosting. This is for minds that don't have somewhere to go. 

**SPACE**
- Galactic travel would also be cool, since you could upload one into a ship and have its job be to manage all of the systems. It could be active when humans can't, since they don't need food or water.
