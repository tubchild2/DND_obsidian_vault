#### Atoms vs Molecules
**Atoms**
- Zinc and iron are composed of atoms

**Molecules**
- Hydrogen, nitrogen, and fluorine are diatomic
- A collection of atoms.

**Diatomic Molecules**
- 2 Atoms

**Elements**
- One type of atom
- Zinc, iron, and hydrogen are pure elements

**Compound**
- Two or more different types of atoms
- Sodium Chloride (NaCl) and water (H2O) contain two types of atoms

**Ionic Compounds**
- NaCl is an ionic compound
- Sodium is a metal and chlorine is a nonmetal
- *Usually* made up of metals and nonmetals
- Made up of oppositely charged ions held together by ionic bonds

**Molecular Compounds**
- CO2 is a molecular compound
- Carbon is a nonmetal and oxygen is a nonmetal
- *Usually* made up of nonmetals bonded to each other
- Formed when nonmetal atoms share valence electrons to create covalent bonds

**Metals**
- Metalloids are the dividing line between metals and nonmetals
- The left metalloids are metals, the right metalloids are nonmetals
	- Boron, silicon, arsenic, etc. are nonmetals
	- Aluminum, germanium, antimony, etc. are metals
- Usually contain cations

**Nonmetals**
- Usually contain anions

**Ion**
- Atom or molecule
- Net electrical charge
- Inequal number of protons and electrons

**Cations**
- Positively charged ions

**Anions**
- Negatively charged ions

**Ionic Bonds**
- Type of chemical bond
- Formed through electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions

**Covalent Bonds**
- Type of chemical bond
- Formed when two nonmetal atoms share valence electron pairs to attain a stable, full, outer electron shell


#### Naming Compounds
**Elements Written Form**
- Element symbol (H, Li, Be, etc.)
- Number. Represents how many of that atom are in the molecule

**Element Count Prefixes**
- Represents the number of atoms of a given element in the compound
- You can usually disregard mono in the first element
- You don't use prefixes in ionic compounds
	- If need be, you use Roman Numerals to represent the oxidation state
	- Be careful with polyatomic compounds, because they aren't as predictable 
	- FeCl3
		- 3 Chloride = -3 charge
		- Iron needs a +3 charge to be neutral
		- Iron (III) Chloride
	- Cu2SO4
		- 2 Copper + 1 Sulfate Ion
		- Sulfate has a charge of -2
		- Copper (I) Sulfate
- Mono -> 1
- Di -> 2
- Tri -> 3
- Tetra -> 4
- Penta -> 5
- Hexa -> 6
- Hepta -> 7
- Octa -> 8
- Nona -> 9
- Deca -> 10

**Compound Length**
- Represents the number of elements in the compound
- Ide -> 2

**Polyatomic Compounds**
- Molecules with multiple atoms
- Have unique names, charges, and are enclosed with parentheses

**Names to Formulas**
- Reverse engineer prefixes, whether they're ionic or molecular compounds, and any known polyatomic compounds
- Ensure net charge is 0
- Aluminum Sulfate --> Al2(SO4)3

#### Isotopes
**Protons**
	Equal to the atomic number
	Positively charged

**Neutrons**
	Equal to the mass number minus the atomic number
	Neutrally charged

**Electrons**
	Equal to the atomic number minus the charge 
	Negatively charged
	Cations have more protons than electrons
	Anions have more electrons than protons

**Isotope**
	A variant of an element with a different number of neutrons
	Carbon 12 is an isotope of carbon.
	The number of the isotope (the 12 in Carbon 12) represents the new total mass
