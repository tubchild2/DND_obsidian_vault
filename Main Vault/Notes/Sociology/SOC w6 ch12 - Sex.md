## Sex, Gender, Identity, and Expression
**Sex**
	Refers to physical or physiological differences between males and females. 
	Includes both reproductive system and secondary characteristics such as height and muscularity. 

**Gender**
	Behaviors, personal traits, and social positions that society attributes to being male or female. 

**Berdache**
	Refers to individuals who occasionally or permanently dressed and lived as a different gender. 

**Sexuality**
	Their capacity to experience sexual feelings and attraction. 
	Cultures have different attitudes about premarital sex, the age of consent, homosexuality, masturbation, etc.

**Sexual Orientation**
	Physical, mental, emotional, and sexual attraction a particular sex (male and/or female). 
	**Heterosexuality**
		Attraction to individuals of the opposite sex
	**Homosexuality / Gay / Lesbian**
		Attraction to individuals of the same sex
	**Bisexuality**
		Attraction to individuals of either sex
	**Asexuality**
		Lack of sexual attraction
	**Pansexuality**
		Attraction to people regardless of sex, gender, gender identity, or gender expression
	**Omnisexuality**
		Attraction to people of all sexes, genders, gender identities, and gender expressions
	**Queer**
		An umbrella term to describe sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression.
	**Aromantic**
		Doesn't experience romantic attraction
	**Demisexual**
		Someone who feels a sexual attraction to someone only after they form an emotional bond
	**Homosocial**
		Nonsexual same-sex relationships

**Heteronormative Societies**
	People assume heterosexual orientation is biologically determined and is the default or normal type of orientation. 


#### Gender Roles
**Gender Role**
	Refers to society's concept of how men and women are expected to look and how they should behave. 

**Masculine Roles**
	Associated with strength, aggression, and dominance. 
	Usually kids play with things like trucks, toy guns, and superhero paraphernalia

**Feminine Roles**
	Associated with passivity, nurturing, and subordination. 
	Usually kids play with dolls and dress-up apparel. 

**Occupational Sorting**
	Men tend to outnumber women in professions such as law enforcement, the military, and politics. 
	Women outnumber men in care-related occupations such as childcare, healthcare, and social work. 

**Motherhood Penalty v. the Fatherhood Premium**
	Women have a hard time shedding the expectation that they can't be a good mother and a good worker.
	Men don't have this problem at all.


#### Gender Identity
**Gender Identity**
	A person's deeply held internal perception of one's gender. 

**Transgender**
	A person who identifies and/or lives as a gender opposite to their birth assigned gender. 
	Doesn't indicate sexual orientation or a particular gender expression. 

**Cisgender**
	Identifying with the sex you were assigned with at birth. 

**Intersex**
	Describe people whose sex traits, reproductive anatomy, hormones, or chromosomes are different from the usual two ways human bodies develop. 

**Gender Dysphoria**
	Diagnostic category that describes a deep sense of unease, anxiety, or discomfort that may occur in people whose gender doesn't align with their sex assigned at birth. 

**Heterosexism**
	Both an ideology and a set of institutional practices that privilege straight people and heterosexuality over other sexual orientations. 

**Homophobia**
	An extreme or irrational aversion to LGBTQ people, which manifests as prejudice and bias. 

**Transphobia**
	Fear, hatred, or dislike of transgender people. 


## Gender and Gender Inequality
#### Gender and Socialization
**Gender Stereotyping**
	There are distinct expectations for boys and girls. 
	Oversimplified notions about members of a group. 

**Sexism**
	Prejudiced beliefs that value one sex over another. 
	Occurs at both the micro and macro levels. 

**Institutional Discrimination**
	Discrimination built into social structure. 

**Agents of Gender Socialization**
	Family
		Parents socialize sons and daughters differently. 
		Girls are given more freedom to step outside their prescribed gender role.
		Boys are given more autonomy and independence at an earlier age
	Education
		Until very recently, schools were explicit in their efforts to stratify boys and girls. 
		Girls were encouraged to take home economics or humanities
		Boys were encouraged to take math and science
	Peer Groups
		Mimicking the actions of others is the first step in the development of a unique sense of self
		Children become agents and actively facilitate and apply normative gender expectations to those around them.
		Girls who want to take Karate may be called a "tomboy"
	Mass Media
		In TV and movies, women tend to have less significant roles and are often portrayed as wives or mothers.
		When given a lead role, it often falls into one of two extremes: a wholesome, saint-like figure, or a malevolent, hypersexual figure. 
	Religion
	Workplace

#### Feminism
**Misogyny**
	The hatred of, aversion to, or prejudice against women. 

**The Feminine Mystique**
	Contested the post-WW2 belief that it was women's sole destiny to marry and bear children. 
	Many women agreed that homemaking in the suburbs sapped them of their individualism and left them unsatisfied. 

**Feminism**
	Movement with the goal to make possible women's participation in all aspects of American life and to gain for them all the rights enjoyed by men.

**1970s Feminist Movements**
	Battered Women's shelters
	Fought unemployment discrimination for pregnant women
	Reformed rape laws (like laws requiring witness corroboration)
	Criminalized domestic violence
	Funded schools that sought to counter sexist stereotypes 
	Roe v. Wade invalidated state laws under which abortions obtained during the first 3 months of pregnancy were illegal

**Gloria Steinem**
	Journalist and prominent advocate for women's rights. 
	Founded Ms. Magazine. 


## Perspectives
**Functionalist**
- Views the family as the most integral component of society
- Assumptions about gender roles within marriage assume a prominent place in this perspective
- Argues gender roles were established before the pre-industrial era when men typically took care of responsibilities outside of the home. 
	- They were established because women were often physically constrained to the home because of things like pregnancy and nursing. 
- In WW2, changes in the family structure also occurred. Many women had to assume the role of breadwinner alongside their domestic role. When men returned, society fell back into a state of imbalance. 

**Conflict Theorist**
- Society is a struggle for dominance among social groups, like men vs women that compete for scarce resources. 
- Social problems are created when dominant groups exploit or oppress subordinate groups. 
- Friedrich Engels suggested that the same owner-worker relationship seen in the labor force is also seen in the household, with women assuming the role of the proletariat. 

**Feminist Theory**
- Type of conflict theory that examines inequalities in gender-related issues.
- Examine the maintenance of gender roles and inequalities.
- **Radical Feminism**
	- Wants to uproot the patriarchy
	- Considers the role of the family in perpetuating male dominance

**Symbolic Interactionism**
- Discusses the critical role of masculine and feminine symbols in human interaction. 

**Doing Gender**
- When people perform tasks or possess characteristics based on the gender role assigned to them.

**Social Construction of Sexuality**
- The way in which socially created definitions about the cultural appropriateness of sex-linked behavior shape the way people see and experience sexuality

**Biological Determinism**
- The belief that men and women behave differently due to differences in their biology. 

## Sexuality
**Cultural Universality**
	Sexuality is a cultural universal. The vast majority of human beings have participated in sexual relationships. 
	Each society interprets sexuality and sexual activity in different ways. 

**Incest**
	Taboo in every society, though which relative is deemed taboo varies. 

**Chastity**
	Valued in societies like China, Iran, and India. 
	Not as valued in places like France, the Netherlands, and Sweden. 

**Sexuality in Sweden**
	In Sweden, they are extremely open about it. 
	There are very few regulations on sexual images, and education begins at around age 6. 
	It's helped prevent teen pregnancies and STDs. 

**Sexuality in the US**
	US culture is highly restrictive in its attitudes about sex when it comes to women and sexuality. 
	There's a double standard, because men are presumed to be more sexual, so they're allowed to engage in sexual relationships with as many partners as they want, but women are only allowed to engage in sex with partners in committed love relationships. 

**Sexual Thoughts in Men vs Women**
	It's widely believed that men are more sexual than women.
	Research suggests men think about sex an average of 19 times per day, which is closer to once per hour.
	Research suggests women think about sex 10 times per day. 

**US Sex Ed**
	Not required in all public school curricula in the US.
	Controversial not for its presence, but for the type of sex ed that should be taught. 
	Abstinence vs comprehensive sex ed is the primary debate. 

**Health Trends**
	Ages of first menstruation and breast development are continually declining in the US. 
	Education about these changes typically doesn't begin until middle school, which is already too late.

**Functionalist Perspective**
	Functionalists stress the importance of regulating sexual behavior to ensure marital cohesion and familial stability. 
	Talcott Parsons (1955) argued that the regulation of sexual activity is an important function of the family. 
	The purpose of encouraging sexual activity in the confines of marriage is to intensify the bond between spouses and to ensure that procreation occurs within a stable, legally recognized relationship.

**Conflict Theorist Perspective**
	Sexuality is another area in which power differentials are present and where dominant groups actively work to promote their worldview. 
	Economically, marriage brings certain entitlements, like Social Security benefits, and medical insurance. 

**Symbolic Interactionist Perspective**
	Focus on the meanings associated with sexuality and sexual orientation. 
	Cooley's looking glass self suggests that the self develops as a result of our interpretation and evaluation of the responses of others. Exposure to homophobia would lead to a negative self-image, or worse, self-hate. 
	Homosexual youths are six times more likely to have high levels of depression and eight times more likely to have attempted suicide. 

**Queer Theory**
	Interdisciplinary approach to sexuality studies. 
	Identifies Western society's rigid splitting of gender into specific roles.
	Questions the manner in which we've been taught to think about sexual orientation. 
	Focuses on the mismatch between sex, gender identity, and sexual orientation. 
	Their argument that sexuality is more than just the sex of someone's desired partner
		- Identical genital acts mean different things to different people
		- Sexuality makes up a large share of the self-perceived identity of some people, and a small share of others
		- Some people spend a lot of time thinking about sex, others little
		- Some people have had a lot of sex, others little or none
		- Many people have their richest mental/emotional involvement with sexual acts they don't do, or don't even want to do. 
		- Some people like spontaneous sexual scenes, others like highly scripted ones, others like spontaneous sounding ones that are nonetheless predictable. 
		- Some people experience sexuality as deeply embedded in a matrix of gender meanings and gender differentials. Others don't.
