### Education Around the World
**Education**
	A social institution through which members of a society are taught basic academic knowledge, learning skills, and cultural norms. 
	Cultural universal. 
	Majorly impacted by resources and money. A country's wealth has much to do with the amount spent on education. 
	Worldwide educational inequality caused by financial differences, varying societal value, varying durations, and varying distributions for education. 

**US Education**
	20% of performance differences and the US' low rankings to differences in social background. 

**Finnish Education**
	Among the best education in the world. 
	Children enter at 7 years old, and have longer breaks and less hours. (300 fewer hours)
	Homework is light.
	There's no gifted programs, private schools, or high-stakes standardized tests.  
	There's an emphasis on:
		- Allocating resources for those who need them the most
		- High standards
		- Support for special needs students
		- Qualified teachers taken from the top 10% of the nation's graduates and who must earn a Master's degree
		- Evaluation of education
	Universities are free
	"Support everyone and don't waste anyone's skills"
	They don't test the teachers or ask them to prove their knowledge, but they do invest in a lot of additional teacher training even after they become teachers. 

**Formal Education**
	The learning of academic facts and concepts through a formal curriculum. 
	Originally only available to the higher classes since they could afford learning materials and had the free time to study. Industrialization made it available to everyone. 

**Informal Education**
	Learning about cultural values, norms, and expected behaviors by participating in a society. 
	Occurs both through formal education and at home. 
	Begins with parents, relatives, and others in our community. 
	Learn important life skills that help us get through the day and interact with each other. 

**Cultural Transmission**
	Refers to the way people come to learn the values, beliefs, and social norms of their culture. 
	Both informal and formal education include cultural transmission. 

**Universal Access**
	Refers to people's equal ability to participate in an education system.
	On a world level, access is more difficult for certain groups based on class or gender. 
	Mills v. Board of Education of the District of Columbia. 
		Brought on the behalf of seven school-age children with special needs who argued the board was denying their access to free public education. 
		The board maintained the children's exceptional needs, precluded their right to be educated for free in a public school setting. The cost of educating them would be too expensive, and so they'd have to remain home without access to education. 
		The judge upheld the student's right to education without a trial.

###### Conflict Theory
View education as a means of widening the gap in social inequality. 
They believe that the educational system reinforces and perpetuates social inequalities that arise from differences in class, gender, race, and ethnicity.

**Social Class**
	The fulfillment of one's education is linked to social class.
	Students of low SES are not afforded the same opportunities, no matter how good they are.
		They have to contend with other struggles and don't have the same amount of free time or energy.

**Pierre Bourdieu**
	Researched how **cultural capital** (cultural knowledge that serves as metaphorical currency that helps us navigate a culture) alters the experiences and opportunities available to students from different social classes. 
	Members of the upper and middle classes have more cultural capital. 

**Hidden Curriculum**
	The type of nonacademic knowledge that students learn through informal learning and cultural transmission. 
	The cycle of rewarding those who possess cultural capital is hidden here and in the **formal curriculum.**

**Tracking**
	A formalized sorting system that places students on tracks (advanced v. low achievers) that perpetuate inequalities. 
	Leads to self-fulfilling prophecies in which students live up or down to a teacher or society's expectations. 

They train working-class students to accept and retain their position as lower members of a society. 
Fulfilled through the disparity of resources available to students in richer and poorer neighborhoods. 

**IQ tests** 
	They've been attacked for being biased. 
	They could ask which instruments belong in an orchestra, which requires cultural knowledge rather than actual intelligence.
	Experts claim bias has been eliminated, conflict theorists maintain that this is impossible. 

**Grade Inflation**
	Grade inflation refers to a practice of awarding students higher grades than they've earned. It reflects the observation that the relationship between letter grades and the achievements they reflect has been changing. What was once considered a C is now a B, or even an A. 
		Some argue grade inflation doesn't exist, or that there are other factors at play. 
	However, there's evidence for the contrary. A widely cited study reveals that the number of A grades has been increasing by several percentage points per decade. 
		In 2019, over 50 people were implicated in a scandal in which wealthy parents illegally secured college admissions for their children. Several people were sentenced to prison. They manipulated test scores, falsified student's academic credentials, and acquired testing accommodations through dishonest claims of having a disability. 
	Some argue it's a product of a culture that rewards effort instead of product.

### Issues in Education
**Segregation**
	Until 1954's Brown v. Board of Education, schools and private businesses allowed racial segregation. 
	State laws that had established separate schools were unequal and unconstitutional. 
	In Arkansas in 1957, the governor mobilized the state National Guard to keep Black Students from entering Little Rock Central High School. President Eisenhower sent members of the 101st Airborne Division from Kentucky to uphold their right to enter the school. 
	In 1963, Governor George Wallace of Alabama used his own body to block two Black students from entering the auditorium at the University of Alabama to enroll. He said, "segregation now, segregation tomorrow, segregation forever." A general from the Alabama National Guard arrived on President Kentucky's order to stop him. 

**Unequal Education**
	Students from wealthy families and those of lower SES don't receive the same opportunities. 
	Public schools are meant to provide universal access. Any race, religion, class, etc.
	**The Coleman Report**
		There's a great divide in performance between white students from affluent backgrounds and their nonwhite, less affluent, counterparts. 
	**Head Start Program**
		Brought about by the Coleman Report
		Gives low-income students an opportunity to make up the preschool deficit discussed in Coleman's findings. 
		Provides academic centered preschool to students of low SES.

**Transfers and Busing**
	In 1958, Activist Mae Mallory, with the support of Ella Baker and the NAACP, led a group of parents who kept their children out of school, essentially boycotting the district. 
	She and the rest of the group, known as the Harlem Nine, were chastised and pursued by the judicial system. 
	After several decisions and appeals, the culminating legal decision found that the city was effectively segregating schools, and that students in certain neighborhoods were still receiving a discriminatorily inferior education. 
	**Busing**
		Involves bringing students to schools outside their neighborhoods, to bring racial diversity into balance.

**No Child Left Behind**
	In 2001, the Bush Administration passed this act. 
	Requires states to test students in grades 3-8 to determine eligibility to receive federal funding. 
	Schools that don't meet the standards run the risk of having their funding cut. 
	Sociologists and teachers contend the impact is far more negative than positive, since there's not one-size fits all fix for education. 
	Altered over time and essentially eliminated in 2015.

**Every Student Succeeds Act**
	ESSA, 2015
	Decreases the federal role in education.
	Annual testing is still required, but the achievement and improvement accountability is shifted to the states. They must submit plans and goals regarding their approaches to the US Department of Education. 

**Teaching to the Test**
	When a curriculum focuses on equipping students to succeed on standardized tests, to the detriment of broader educational goals and concepts of learning. 
	SAT and ACT serve as rites of passage for high school students. 

**Students with Disabilities**
	Since the 1978 implementation of IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act)
	States and increased investment in the quality of education for students with disabilities. 
	Added requirements and guidance for states and school districts. 
	Many students with dyslexia, ADHD, and other disorders are either undiagnosed, not taken seriously, or not given the support they need to succeed. 
	Programs have improved greatly. It's only 15% lower than the average now. 

**School Choice**
	Public school alternatives like vocational schools, special education schools, magnet schools, charter schools, alternative schools, early college schools, and virtual schools. 
	Religious and non-religious private schools, and boarding schools. 
	Homeschooling.
