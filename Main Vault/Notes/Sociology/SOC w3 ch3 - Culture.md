##### Key Points
**Culture**
	Shared values (ideals) 
	Beliefs which strengthen the values
	Norms and rules that maintain the values
	Language so that the values can be taught
	Symbols that form the language people must learn
	Art and artifacts
	The people's collective identities and memories. 

**Community**
	A definable region of society

**Learned Behaviors**
	Almost every human behavior is learned

**Humor**
	Common to all societies, but what's funny isn't. 
	Slapstick may be funny to some, but not to others. 

**Material Culture**
	Physical structures, items you can touch, anything tangible related to culture. 
	Often symbolizes a nonmaterial aspect of culture. 

**Nonmaterial Culture**
	The ideas, attitudes, and beliefs of a society. Anything intangible related to culture.

**Cultural Universals**
	Permanent cultural aspects that do not change across societies. 
	Things like the family unit, music, and humor exist universally, even if they vary in specifics. 


##### Cultural Relativism
**Ethnocentrism**
	To evaluate and judge another culture based on one's own cultural norms. It means believing your group is the correct measuring standard and if other cultures don't measure up to it, that they're wrong. 

**Cultural Imperialism**
	The deliberate imposition of one's own cultural values on another culture. Portugal, Spain, Netherlands, and England did this like crazy in the 15th century. Catholicism, Christianity, European dress, and other practices were enforced in conquered lands. 

**Culture Shock**
	When people find themselves in a new culture and experience disorientation and frustration. 

**Cultural Relativism**
	The practice of assessing a culture by its own standards and requirements rather than viewing it through the lens of one's own culture. It requires having an open mind and a willingness to consider, and even adapt to, new values, norms, and practices. 

**Xenocentrism**
	The opposite of ethnocentrism, and refers to the belief that another culture is superior to one's own. 

**Xenophobia**
	The irrational fear or hatred of different cultures. 




## Elements of Culture
##### Values and Beliefs
**Values**
	Ideals, or principles and standards members of a culture hold in high regard. Values are deeply embedded and are critical for learning a culture's beliefs. 

**Beliefs**
	The tenets or convictions that people hold to be true. The belief is the American Dream, a value behind that is the importance of wealth.

**Collectivism**
	The belief that the needs of many come before the needs of one. 

**Ideal Culture**
	The standards society would like to embrace and live up to. 

**Real Culture**
	The actual culture society produces that's independent from their ideal culture. 

**Sanctions / Social Control**
	Forms of social control. Ways to encourage conformity to cultural norms or rules. 


##### Norms
**Norms**
	Behaviors that reflect compliance with what cultures and societies have defined as good, right, and important. Most members adhere to them. 
	Formal norms are established, written rules existing in all societies. Laws are formal norms. 
	Informal norms are causal behaviors that are generally and widely conformed to. Some are taught directly, and others are learned through observation. 

**Social Institutions**
	Military, criminal justice, healthcare systems, and public schools are examples of social institutions. 

**Sanctions**
	A way to authorize or formally disapprove of certain behaviors. 

**Mores (mor-ays)**
	Norms that embody the moral views and principles of a group. They often have a religious foundation.
	Violating them can have serious consequences. The strongest mores are protected with laws and other formal sanctions. 

**Folkways**
	Norms without any moral underpinnings. They direct appropriate behavior in the day-to-day practiices and expressions of a culture. 
	We can think of them as traditions; things we do because we always have. 
	I think of them like rivers that have slowly been carved over time. It's now the easier way for the water to flow since it's so deeply entrenched and so many structures have been built around the expectation of it. 


##### Symbols
**Symbols**
	Symbols provide communication methods to understanding experiences by conveying recognizable meanings that are shared by societies. They're physical culture that can convey nonmaterial cultural meanings. 

**Semaphore Signals**
	A way of long distance communication using two flags. The semaphore signals for Nuclear Disarmament (ND) form the peace symbol. 


##### Language
**Language**
	A system that uses symbols with which people communicate and through which culture is transmitted. 	Letters, pictographs, and hand gestures are all symbols that create a language used for communication. 
	All language systems contain basic elements to represent objects, subjects, and actions. 
	Written languages contain symbols that refer to spoken sounds. 
	New words are created to represent ideas constantly.

**Sapir-Whorf Hypothesis** (or Linguistic Relativity)
	Based on the idea that people experience their world through their language, and therefore understand their world through the cultural meanings embedded in their language. 

**Gendered Language Study**
	Some languages assign genders to insentient objects. One study found that gender perceptions carried over into how they perceive gendered objects. This meant that language did fundamentally change how people saw the world. 

**Language Experience Limitations Conjecture**
	The Sapir-Whorf hypothesis has been interpreted to suggest that if a word does not exist in a language then users of that language cannot have that experience. 

**Triskaidekaphobia**
	The fear of the number 13, and why high-rise buildings don't include a 13th floor. 

**Ambivalence**
	Simultaneously having conflicting feelings. 




## High, Low, Pop, Sub, and Counter Culture
**High Culture**
	Describes the pattern of cultural experiences and attitudes that exist in the highest or elite class segments of society. 
	Associated with intellectualism, political power, and prestige. In the US, it's also associated with wealth, which is why high culture experiences tend to be more expensive. 

**Low Culture**
	Associated with the pattern of cultural experiences and attitudes that exist in the lowest class segments of society. 

**Pop Culture**
	The pattern of cultural experiences and attitudes that exist in mainstream society. 
	Accessible by most.  
	Expressed and spread via commercial and social media outlets such as radio, television, movies, the music industry, publishers, and corporate-run websites. 

**Sub Culture**
	A smaller cultural group within a larger culture. Ethnic / racial groups, groups formed through shared experiences, groups formed by people who possess traits or preferences that differ from the majority of a society's population, etc.
	They still identify with and participate in the larger society. 
	It's more than just a shared interest. 

**Countercultures**
	Smaller cultural groups within a larger culture that reject some of the larger culture's norms and values. They might actively defy larger society by developing their own set of rules and norms to live by. 
	Cults are countercultures. 


##### Cultural Change
**Cultural Change**
	Cultures change because new items are added to material culture every day, and in turn, meanings are assigned to them. 
	Material cultural change happens when new items are discovered or invented or enter a culture as a result of globalization. 

**Innovation**
	A type of cultural change. Refers to an object or concept's initial appearance in society. 
	Discoveries make known previously unknown but existing aspects of reality. 
	Inventions result when something new is formed from existing objects or concepts. 

**Innovation Adoption**
	Innovators adopt the change first
	Early adopters are next
	Then the early majority
	Then the late majority
	And then the laggards

**Generation Gaps**
	Formed when the pace of innovation increases. The older generation might refuse or struggle to adopt new innovations. 

**Culture Lag**
	The time that passes between the introduction of a new item of material culture and its social acceptance.  

**Diffusion**
	A type of cultural change. Like a gas in a laboratory, the item or idea spreads throughout. 




## Theoretical Perspectives on Culture
**Structural Functionalist**
	Societies need culture to exist. Cultural norms support the fluid operation of society, and cultural values guide people in making choices. 

**Conflict Theorist**
	They view social structure as inherently unequal. Cultural education methods are seen as reinforcing the dominant societal culture and issues of privilege. 

**Symbolic Interactionalist**
	Most concerned with face-to-face interactions and cultural meanings between members of society. They study how people perceive reality based on their environment and interactions. They study languages. They study additions and deletions of words, the changing meaning of words, and the transmission of words in an original language into different ones.
