	The social institution through which a society's resources are exchanged and managed.
	Based on the exchange of goods and services so individuals can get meet their needs and wants. 

**Mechanical Solidarity**

**Organic Solidarity**
	Arises out of mutual interdependence created by the specialization of work.

## Economic Systems
###### Early Economies
**Wild Cereals**
	Other term for edible grains and grasses

**Hunter Gatherer**
	Didn't have an economy. Families just moved from place to place looking for sustenance. 

**The Agricultural Revolution**
	Disagreement on the actual timeline
	Egypt and Mesopotamia: 11000-10000 BCE
	Asia: 9000 BCE
	New Guinea: 8000-6000 BCE
	Sub-Saharan Africa: 5000 BCE
	The Americas: 4000-3000 BCE

**The Plow**
	Agriculture took off when the plow was invented since it let you harness animals to grow enough crops for multiple people, instead of just for one person. 
	People could abandon the nomadic life and settle down to farm. 

**Villages and Towns**
	As farming production increased, new jobs emerged, along with new technologies. 
	Economies began because people had goods and services to trade. 
	Villages grew into towns, and then into cities. 
	Urban areas created the need for administrators and public servants. 
	Disputes over ownership, payments, debts, etc. created the need for laws and courts. 

**Bartering**
	Exchanging one form of goods or services for another.
	Only works when two people have what the other person needs.

**Money**
	An object society agrees to assign value to so it can be exchanged for payment. 
	In early economies, it was often objects like cowry shells, rice, barley, or even rum. 
	Precious metals became the preferred means because of their durability and portability. 
	The first coins were minted in Lydia in around 650-600 BCE

**Mercantilism**
	An economic policy based on accumulating silver and gold by controlling colonial and foreign markets through taxes and other charges. 
	Led to monopolies, bans on certain goods, high tariffs, and exclusivity requirements. 
	Promoted manufacturing, and helped to create the equipment that led to the Industrial Revolution.

**Industrial Inventions**
	Cotton Jenny
	Coke from coal being used in all stages of smelting iron
	James Watt's improved steam engine
	Assembly line jobs
	6 times the world's population were invented between 1800 and 2000
	Per capita income went up tenfold (invention)
	Led to lots of inequalities between owners and laborers.
	Worker's rights, wage protection, and safe work environments are still issues.

**Subsistence Farming**
	Practiced prior to the industrial revolution
	They produced only enough to feed themselves and pay their taxes.

**Four Sectors of an Economy**
	As economies develop, they move through each of these sectors.
	Underdeveloped countries specialize in the primary sector. 
	Then, as they develop, more people move to the secondary sector.
	Well developed economies focus on the tertiary sector.
	**Primary Sector**
		Extracts and produces raw materials like metals and crops
	**Secondary Sector**
		Turns those raw materials into finished goods.
	**Tertiary Sector**
		Provides services
	**Quaternary Sector**
		Produces ideas. Information and the means to use it creatively. 

###### Capitalism
**Capitalism**
	An economic system in which there is private ownership (as opposed to state ownership), and where there is an impetus to produce profit, and thereby wealth. 
	In a capitalist economy, people invest capital into businesses to produce products or services that can be sold in a market to consumers. 

**The Law of Supply and Demand**
	When demand exceeds supply, prices rise.
	When supply exceeds demand, prices fall.

**Competition**
	When multiple businesses market similar products and services to the same buyers.

**Wages**
	Also obey the law of supply and demand.
	Competition helps determine how much people will be paid.

**Monopolies**
	During early capitalism in the US, corporations got massive and controlled every stage of production and used their enormous wealth to destroy their competition.
	In the 1800s, the US passed laws to break up monopolies and set laws about how key industries could conduct business.

**US Capitalism**
	It is capitalist, but the government still has major influence on private companies through laws, regulations, taxes, guidelines, etc.


###### Socialism
**Socialism**
	An economic system in which there is government ownership of goods and their production, with an impetus to share work and wealth equally among the members of a society. 
	Everything people produce (both goods and services) are considered social products.
	People who produce social products are entitled to a share in any benefits that come from its sale or use. 
	Focuses on benefitting society. 
	Began as a reaction to uncontrolled industrial capitalism in the 1800s and 1900s.

**Views on the Extent of Socialist Government Economic Control**
	Some believe all the most personal items are public property.
	Others believe only essential services need direct control (healthcare, education, utilities, etc.)

**Views on the Extent of Socialist Government Control Exertion**
	Communist countries like the USSR, China, Vietnam, and North Korea had the national government control the economy centrally. They had the power to tell all businesses what to produce, how much to produce, and what to charge for it.
	Other socialists believe control should be decentralized so it can be exerted by those most affected by the industries being controlled. Like a town collectively owning and managing the businesses on which its residents depend. 

**Market Socialism**
	A subtype of socialism that adopts certain traits of capitalism, like allowing limited private ownership or consulting market demands.
	Key industries are nationalized and directly controlled by the government, but most businesses are privately owned and regulated by the government. 

**Mutualism**
	Individuals and cooperative groups exchange products with one another on the basis of mutually satisfactory contracts.

**Convergence Theory**
	Argues that as a country's economy grows, its societal organization changes to become more like that of an industrialized society.
	People move from job to job and conditions improve and opportunities arise.
	Supporters point to Germany, France, and Japan in the 1960s and 1970s, and how they converged with countries with developed economies and are now considered developed economies themselves. 
	**THIS CONFUSES ME**
	Developing countries need to be able to attract inexpensive capital to invest in new businesses and improve traditionally low productivity. They need new international markets for buying the goods. If they lack that, then their economies can't catch up. 
	Some technology in new countries can be implemented much faster since they don't have to go through all the same stages of invention and development older countries did. Telephone systems are a lot easier to establish now than they were 100 years ago. 


###### Theoretical Perspectives
**Functionalist Perspective**
	They view work and the economy as a well-oiled machine that is designed for maximum efficiency. 
	Assumes that the health of the economy is vital to the health of the nation. 
	Dysfunctions may occur and disrupt social institutions or organizations. This can be caused by some institutions failing to adapt quickly to changing social conditions, like the bursting of the housing bubble in 2008.
	Functionalists argue that market forces fluctuate between excess, inflation, and recession. In reality, to control the risk of an economic depression, the US government has to adjust interest rates to encourage more lending. So, letting the natural cycle fluctuate isn't a gamble most governments are willing to take. 

**Excess**
	A phase of the market where there is a surplus of market goods.

**Inflation**
	When the money buys less than it did yesterday

**Recession**
	Which occurs when there are two or more consecutive quarters of economic decline

**Depression**
	Sustained recession across several economic sectors.

**Conflict Perspective**
	Sees the economy not as a source of stability, but rather as a means of reflecting and reproducing economic inequality, particularly in a capitalist marketplace. 
	Classically Marxist, with the bourgeoisie accumulating wealth and power by exploiting and perhaps oppressing the proletariat, and regulating those who can't work into the great mass of unemployed. 
	They believe wealth is concentrated in the hands of those who don't deserve it. 

**Symbolic Perspective**
	They talk about career inheritance, career socialization, and job satisfaction.

**Career Inheritance**
	Means simply that children tend to enter the same or similar occupations as their parents.
	Children learns the norms and values that helped their parents succeed in their jobs, and then see the model career path, making their parents jobs even more attractive.

**Career Socialization**
	Learning the norms and values of a particular job.

**Job Satisfaction**
	Melvin Kohn and his fellow researchers determined that workers were more likely to be happy when they 
		A) believe they controlled some part of their work
		B) Felt they were part of the decision making processes associated with their work
		C) When they have freedom from surveillance
		D) When they felt integral to the outcome of their work
	Sunyal, Sunyal, and Yasin (2011) found that a greater sense of vulnerability to stress, the more stress experienced by a worker, and a greater amount of perceived risk consistently predicted to a lower worker job satisfaction. 


## Globalization and the Economy
**Globalization**
	The process of integrating governments, cultures, and financial markets through international trade into a single world market. 
	Begins with a single motive, like market expansion, but usually, there's a snowball effect, and it becomes a mixed bag of economic, philanthropic, entrepreneurial, and cultural efforts.

**North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA)**
	Among the countries of North America, and allowed much freer trade opportunities without the kind of tarrifs and import laws that restrict international trade. 
	Thought it would lead to major gains in jobs in 1994.
	Actually led to unemployment in Mexico, which spurred greater amounts of illegal immigration motivated by a search for work. 
	Renegotiated in 2018, and was replaced by the United States Mexico Canada Agreement

**Panacea**
	A supposed cure for all diseases or a universal solution to all problems. 
	Often carries a skeptical tone, implying a remedy or fix that is overly hyped or unrealistic. 

**Multinational Corporations**
	A large share of their capital is collected from a variety of different nations
	Their business is conducted without regard to national borders
	They concentrate wealth in the hands of core nations and already healthy individuals, and they play a key role in the global economy.

**Global Assembly Lines**
	Where products are assembled over the course of several international transactions. 

**Global Commodity Chains**
	Where internationally integrated economic links connect workers and corporations for the purpose of manufacture and marketing. 
	In maquiladoras, mostly in northern Mexico, workers may sew imported precut pieces of fabric into garments. 

**Globalist Xenophobia**
	Globalization brings an international division of labor, in which comparatively wealthy workers from core nations compete with the low-wage labor pool of peripheral and semi-peripheral nations. 
	This can lead to a sense of xenophobia. 

**Early Globalization**
	Greece and Rome traded with Africa, the Middle East, India, and China.
	The Islamic Golden Age expanded trade even further after the rise of the Mongol empire.
	Colonial empires after the voyages of discovery by European countries meant that trade was going on all over the world. 

**Pros of Globalization**
	Exponentially accelerated progress of development
	Creation of international awareness and empowerment
	Potential for increased wealth. 

**Cons of Globalization**
	Critics worry about the growing influence of enormous international financial and industrial corporations that benefit most from free trade and unrestricted markets. They fear they'll use their wealth and resources to control governments to act in their interest rather than that of the local population. 
	Other critics worry about impacts on the environment and on local economies. 
	Critics say it leads to weak labor movements.
	Critics are concerned that wealthy countries can force economically weaker nations to open their markets while protecting their own local products from competition.
