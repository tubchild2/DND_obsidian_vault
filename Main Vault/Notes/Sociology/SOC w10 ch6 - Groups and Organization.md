## Types of Groups
**Group**
	Amorphous term that can refer to a wide variety of gatherings.
	2+ people who interact with some frequency and who share a sense that their identity is somehow aligned with the group.

**Aggregate**
	People who happen to be in the same place at the same time but who don't interact or share a sense of identity. 
	Also called a crowd. 

**Category**
	A nongroup of people who share similar characteristics but aren't tied to each other in any way. 

**Primary Groups**
	Play the most critical role in our lives. 
	Usually fairly small and made up of individuals who generally engage face-to-face in long-term emotional ways. 
	Usually made up of significant others, those who have the most impact on our socialization. 
	The best example is the family. 
	Serves expressive functions.

**Secondary Groups**
	Larger and more impersonal. 
	May be task-focused and time-limited. 
	Classrooms are examples of secondary groups. 
	Serves instrumental functions. 

**Expressive Functions**
	Serves a role that is more emotional oriented than pragmatic. 

**Instrumental Function**
	Serves a role that is more goal or task oriented than emotional. 

**In Groups**
	The group that an individual feels they belong to, and feels it to be an integral part of who they are. 

**Out Groups**
	A group someone doesn't belong to.

**Reference Groups**
	Groups that people compare themselves to. 
	Cultural centers, workplaces, family gatherings, and even parents. 

## Group Size and Structure
**Dyads**
	Two member group
	Ends if someone withdraws, like a divorce, or a close friendship ending

**Triads**
	Three member group
	Doesn't end if someone withdraws
	Majority opinions can form on any issue
	Two against one dynamics can develop

**Small Group**
	One where the collection of people is small enough that all the members of the group know each other and share simultaneous interaction, such as a nuclear family, a dyad, or a triad. 
	Strong internal cohesiveness and a sense of connection. 
	May struggle to achieve large goals. 

**Large Groups**
	Perhaps it occurs when one group grows so large that there are too many people to join in a simultaneous discussion, or when one group joins with other groups as part of a movement. 
	May share a geographic space, or may be spread out across the globe. 

**Leaders**
	Large groups usually require some kind of leadership
	Small groups usually have informal leadership. 
	Secondary groups usually have highly structured and clearly understood chains of command. 

**Leadership Function**
	Refers to the main goal of the leader, which may be instrumental or expressive. 

**Instrumental Leader**
	One who is goal oriented and largely concerned with accomplishing set tasks. 
	Usually stereotyped as men.

**Expressive Leader**
	One who is more concerned with promoting emotional strength and health, and ensuring that people feel supported. 
	Usually stereotyped as women.

**Democratic Leaders**
	Encourage group participation in all decision making. 
	Work hard to build consensus before choosing a course of action and moving forward. 
	Very common and well liked, but at risk of slow decision making and the formation of factions rather than making a decision. 

	You could talk about these

**Laissez-Faire Leaders**
	Hands-off, allowing group members to self-manage and make their own decisions. 
	Can work well with highly motivated and mature participants who have clear goals and guidelines, it risks group dissolution and a lack of progress.

**Authoritarian Leaders**
	Issue orders and assign tasks with little to no feedback from group members. 
	Often instrumental leaders with a strong focus on meeting goals. 
	Authoritarian leaders risk alienating the workers, but is fast and highly informed by the leader's experience. 

**Women Leaders**
	The public expects and prefers qualities from its leaders and expects and prefers qualities based on the candidates gender. For female presidential candidates, these expectations often conflict. 

**Conformity**
	The extent to which an individual complies with group norms or expectations. 
	Depends on how much you like being noticed, or how much you like to conform to group norms. 

**Solomon Asch**
	Made a fake experimentation group to test how suggestible people were by having everyone choose obviously false answers. 35/50 people responded with an obviously wrong answer at least once. 
	People were more likely to give a correct answer when outnumbered if they had at least one ally. 
	People were more likely to challenge when there was only one opponent. 

**Stanley Milgram**
	Yale psychologist
	Had similar results with the Milgram Experiment
	Subjects were overwhelmingly willing to perform acts that directly conflicted with their consciences when directed by a person of authority. 

**The Bystander Effect**
	A situation in which people are less likely to interfere during an emergency or if a social norm is being violated if there are others around. 
	They feel less responsible because of the presence of other bystanders. 
	This is known as **diffusion of responsibility**. 
	People say they don't want to get involved and that's why they don't respond when they see something wrong. 


## Formal Organizations
**Normative Organizations / Voluntary Organizations**
	Based on shared interests.
	Joining them is voluntary. 
	People find membership rewarding in an intangible way. 
	They receive non-material benefits. 

**Coercive Organizations**
	Groups that we must be coerced, or pushed, to join. 
	Prisons or rehabilitation centers. 
	Erving Goffman stated that most coercive organizations are **total institutions**, which is one in which inmates live a controlled lifestyle in which total resocialization takes place. 

**Utilitarian Organization**
	Groups that are joined because of the need for a specific material reward. 
	High schools and workplaces fall into this category. 

|Normative or Voluntary|Coercive|Utilitarian|
|---|---|---|
|Benefit of Membership|Intangible benefit|Corrective benefit|Tangible benefit|
|Type of Membership|Volunteer basis|Required|Contractual basis|
|Feeling of Connectedness|Shared affinity|No affinity|Some affinity|
**Bureaucracies**
	Max Weber characterized a bureaucracy as having a hierarchy of authority, a clear division of labor, explicit rules, and impersonality. 
	People often complain about them, calling them slow, rule-bound, difficult to navigate, and unfriendly. 
	Also often meritocracies. Hiring and promotion is based on proven and documented skills, rather than nepotism or random choice. 
	Improve efficiency, ensure equal opportunities, and serve a large population. 

**Hierarchy of Authority**
	The chain of command that places one individual or office in charge of another. 

**Clear Division of Labor**
	Each individual has a specialized task to perform

**Explicit Rules**
	Rules that are outlined, written down, and standardized.

**Impersonality**
	Takes personal feelings out of professional situations. 
	Grows to some extent out of a desire to avoid nepotism, backroom deals, and other types of favoritism, while also protecting customers and others served by the organization. 

**Iron Rule of Oligarchy**
	Michels (1911) suggested that all large organizations are characterized by this.
	Wherein an entire organization is ruled by a few elites. 

**The McDonaldization of Society (1993)**
	The increasing presence of the fast food business model in common social institutions.
	High efficiency, predictability, calculability, and control. 

**DeMcDonaldization**
	Farmers markets, microbreweries, and various DIY trends. 

**Merger Mania**
	A bunch of healthcare providers merged together in the 1990s.
