##### Key Points
**Definition of Sociology**
The scientific and systematic study of groups and group interactions, societies and social interactions, from small and personal groups to very large groups. I'd define it as the study of groups of people. 

**Culture vs Society**
A society is a large group of people with a shared culture. 
A culture is the product of and beliefs of that group of people.

**Micro and Macro Sociology**
Micro-level sociological analysis looks at small groups and individual interactions. Micro-level theories look at very specific relationships between individuals or small groups. 
Macro-level sociological analysis looks at trends among large groups and societies. Macro-level theories relate to large scale issues and large groups of people.

**Cultural Patterns**
Social forces and influences that put pressure on people to select one choice over another.

**Social Facts**
The laws, morals, values, religious beliefs, customs, fashions, rituals, and cultural rules that govern social life. 

**Figuration**
The process on simultaneously analyzing the behavior of individuals and the society that shapes them. It's the idea that the individual and society are inseparable. They feed off each other.

**Social Institutions**
A powerful social force in a society and culture. 

**Quantitative Sociology**
Uses statistical methods such as surveys with large numbers of participants. Researches analyze data using statistical techniques to see if they can uncover patterns of human behavior. 

**Qualitative Sociology**
Seeks to understand human behavior by learning about it through in-depth interviews, focus groups, and analysis of content sources (like books, magazines, journals, and popular media).

**The Chicago School of Sociology**
Also known as the Ecological School. It's a school of thought that places much importance on the environment in which relationships and behaviors develop.

**The Looking-Glass Self**
Posited by Charles Horton Cooley. He argued that individuals compare themselves to others in order to check themselves against social standards and remain part of the group. He believed that we see ourselves by the reactions of others with whom we interact. If someone reacts positively to our behavior, theoretically we will continue that behavior. 

**Social Solidarity**
The invisible glue of shared values, belonging, and mutual dependence that holds a society together. 

**Grand Theories**
Attempt to explain large scale relationships and answer fundamental questions such as why societies form and why they change. Accounts for all of society's structure and historical change. 


#### Paradigms
A broad philosophical lens or background assumption used to guide thinking and build theories. Theoretical frameworks used within a discipline to guide theories, generalizations, and the experiments performed in support of them.

**Structural Functionalism**
Focuses on the way each part of society functions together to contribute to the functioning of the whole. Like how each organ works to keep your body healthy or not. Looks at social facts and social institutions like parts of a body. All parts working together to maintain stability is called dynamic equilibrium. Robert Merton pointed out that social processes often have many functions; manifest functions are the consequences of a social process. Undesirable consequences for social processes are called dysfunctions. Some critics say that it can't explain social change even though the functions are processes. Also it doesn't make sense to say that repeated behaviors have a function because they're repeated. 

**Conflict Theory**
Focuses on the way inequities and inequalities contribute to social, political, and power differences and how they perpetuate power. Like the ones with the most toys wins and they will change the rules to the games to keep winning. It views society as a competition for limited resources. Critics show how many social structures are extremely stable and have gradually progressed over time without changing abruptly as conflict theory would suggest. 

**Critical Theory**
An expansion of conflict theory that's broader than just sociology, incorporating other social sciences and philosophy. Holistic theory that attempts to address structural issues causing inequality. 

**Symbolic Interactionism**
Focuses on the way one-on-one interactions and communications behave. Like what's it mean to be an X? A micro-level theory that focuses on the relationships among individuals within a society. George Herbert Mead is considered a founder of symbolic interactionism. 
1. Humans interact with things based on meanings attributed to them. 
2. The ascribed meaning of things comes from our interactions with others and society.
3. The meanings of things are interpreted by a person when dealing with things in specific circumstances.

**Dramaturgical Analysis**
People's interactions showed patterns of cultural "scripts." He argued that individuals were actors in a play. We switched roles, sometimes minute to minute. Because it can be unclear what part a person may play in a given situation, he or she has to improvise his or her role as the situation unfolds. 

**Constructivism**
An extension of symbol interaction theory which proposes that reality is what humans cognitively construct it to be. We develop social constructs based on interactions with others, and those constructs that last over time are those that have meanings which are widely agreed-upon or generally accepted by most within the society. 

**Global Culture**
A culture spanning the entire globe. 

**Positivism**
The scientific study of social patterns

**Antipositivism**
The view that social researchers should strive for subjectivity as they worked to represent social processes, cultural norms, and societal values. 


##### History
**The Catholic Church**
- From the 4th-19th century, the Catholic Church was the seat of power from Turkey to northern Europe.
- In the year 800, Pope Leo III named Charlemagne, the king of Francia, emperor of the Holy Roman Empire, giving one individual significant control over most of Europe. 
- This gave the Church the power to maintain its own traditions and safeguard them from the influence of people practicing other religions.
- If any social patterns challenged the beliefs of the church, those practitioners would be massacred, burned at the stake, or labeled heretics. 

**Ma Tuan-Lin**
- Chinese historian
- 13th century
- First to record the social dynamics underlying and generating historical development.
- Wrote *General Study of Literary Remains*

**Ibn Khaldun**
- Tunisian historian
- 14th century

**Bubonic Plague**
- 14th to 16th century. 
- Damaged the credibility of the Catholic Church. 
- Out of the chaos emerged Copernicus, Galileo, Leonardo, Newton, Linnaeus, and others who sometimes contradicted the Church.
- Human reason and observation explained things that were once held to be the product of the divine hand. 

**Enlightenment**
- 18th century
- John Locke, Francois-Maria Arouet (Voltaire), Immanuel Kant, and Thomas Hobbes
- People were anxious about economic systems, the family, health and hygiene, national offense and defense, women's rights, and more. 

**Industrial Revolution**
- 19th century
- Increased trade, travel, and globalization exposed many people to societies and cultures other than their own. 
- Ideas spread more rapidly, groups were created, and political decisions became public decisions. 

**Auguste Comte**
- Reintroduced the term Sociology which was coined by Emmanuel Joseph Sieyes
- Comte believed in the potential of social scientists to work towards the betterment of society. He thought that once they understood the laws that governed society, they could address problems such as poor education and poverty. 
- The study of social patterns was called positivism. 

**Harriet Martineau**
- Translated Comte to English. 
- Found the workings of capitalism at odds with the professed moral principles of the people in the United States since workers were exploited and impoverished while business owners became wealth, and women were mistreated, both of which contradicted the belief that all were created equal. 

**Karl Marx**
- Him and Friedrich Engels wrote the Communist Manifesto. 
- Rejected Comte's positivism. He believed that societies grew and changed as a result of the struggles of different social classes over the means of production.
- He thought that the inequalities of capitalism would become so extreme that workers would eventually revolt. 

**Herbert Spencer**
- In 1873, wrote the first book with the term sociology in the title. 
- Used Darwinism to suggest the idea of a form of government in which market forces controlled capitalism. 
- His quotes regarding natural selection also went on to be used to justify a whole lot of racism.

**Georg Simmel**
- German art critic. 
- Much of his work focused on micro-level theories, and the dynamics between two and three person groups.

**Max Weber**
- Wrote about Protestants being complementary or preemptive to the rise of capitalism. It was controversial.
- Believed it was impossible to use standard scientific methods to accurately predict the behavior of groups and some sociologists hoped to do. 
- Anti-positivism

**Du Bois**
- Pioneered the use of rigorous empirical methodology into sociology. 
- Studied the African American community and spent his career advocated for equal rights for black people. 

**Thorstein Veblen**
- Unemployed
- Wrote about being unemployed
- Wrote about what 'working' meant

**Jane Addams**
- Founded Hull House, a center that served needy immigrants. 
- Closely associated with the Chicago school of sociology. 

**George Herbert Mead**
- Wrote about how the mind and the self were developed as a result of social processes. 
	- Chicago School of Sociology?
- Adopted Cooley's concept of "looking-glasses," but believed that an individual's reaction to positive or negative reflected depended on who the other was. 
- Significant others had the greatest impact, while generalized others were the organized and generalized attitude of a social group. 

**Robert E. Park**
- The founder of social ecology. 
- Attached to the Chicago School. 
- Focused on how individuals lived within their environment. 
- Researched the inner city to show that no matter who lived there, social chaos was prevalent. As such, it was not the residents who caused the chaos but the environment.
