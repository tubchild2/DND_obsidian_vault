**Robert Merton / Strain Theory**
	Strain theory notes that access to socially acceptable goals plays a part in determining whether a person conforms or deviates. 
	Merton defined five ways that people respond to the gap between having a socially accepted goal and having no socially accepted way to pursue it:
		1. Conformity. Those who conform choose not to deviate. 
			1. They pursue their goals to the extent they can. 
		2. Innovation. They pursue goals they can't reach using criminal or deviant means. 
		3. Ritualism. They lower their goals until they can reach them through acceptable ways. 
		4. Retreatism. They retreat and reject society's goals and means. 
		5. Rebellion. Rebel and replace a society's goals and means with their own. 
			1. Terrorists or freedom fighters.

**Social Disorganization Theory**
	Asserts that crime is most likely to occur in communities with weak social ties and the absence of social control. 
	A person isn't born as someone who will commit crimes, but becomes one over time based on factors in their social environment. 
	Conflict theorists think this oversimplifies social trends.

##### Symbolic Interactionism
**Labelling Theory**
	Examines the ascribing of deviant behavior to another person by members of society. 
	What is deviant is determined by the reactions to those behaviors. 
	People get ascribed a role and internalize and perform it
	Could be countered by fostering a culture of forgiveness. Punishing the sin not the sinner. 

**Edwin Lemert**
	**Primary Deviance.**
		A violation of norms that does not result in any long-term effects on the individual's self-image or interactions with others. 
	**Secondary Deviance.**
		When a person's self-concept and behavior begin to change after his or her actions are labeled as deviant by members of a society. 
		Student cuts class >> labeled deviant >> breaks more rules to fit role
		Some deviances ascribe a master status to someone
	**Master Status**
		A label that describes the chief characteristic of an individual. 

**Neutralization Techniques**
	1. Denial of Responsibility. Doesn't take responsibility or blames others. 
	2. Denial of Injury. Looks at the situation in terms of what effect it has on others. 
	3. Denial of the Victim. If there's no victim, there's no crime. Sees their actions as justified or deserved. 
	4. Condemnation of the Condemners. Turn it around on the accuser by accusing them. 
	5. Appeal to Higher Authority. Claim the actions were for a higher purpose. Making a bad action seem good.

**Felony Disenfranchisement**
	Revoking a criminal's right to vote.
	Criticized for revoking a basic human right. 

**Edwin Sutherland / Differential Association**
	Suggested that individuals learn deviant behavior from those close to them who provide models of and opportunities for deviance. 
	If a person has more exposure to definitions favoring deviant behavior than opposing it, they may be led to partake in deviance.

**Travis Hirschi / Control Theory**
	Stated that social control is directly affected by the strength of social bonds and that deviance results from a feeling of disconnection from society. 
	Travis identified four social bonds that connect people to society. 
		1. Attachment measures our connections to others. When we're close to people, we worry about their opinions of us. People conform to gain approval. 
		2. Commitment refers to the investments we make in the community. A well respected local businessperson with several community obligations has more to lose from committing a crime than a person who doesn't have them. 
		3. Levels of involvement, or participation in socially legitimate activities. 
		4. Belief, an agreement on common values in society. 


## Crime and the Law
**Crime**
	Deviance that is punishable through formal sanctions due to violating official law. 

**Legal Codes**
	Maintain formal social control through laws, which are rules adopted and enforced by a political authority. 


##### Types of Crimes
**Violent Crimes**
	Based on the use of force or the threat of force. 
	Rape, murder, and armed robbery fall under this category. 

**Nonviolent Crimes**
	Involve the destruction or theft of property, but don't use force or the threat of force. 
	Also called property crimes. 

**Street Crime**
	Offenses committed by ordinary people against other people or organizations. 

**Corporate Crime**
	Crime committed by white-collar workers in a business environment. 
	Embezzlement, insider trading, and identity theft. 

**Victimless Crime**
	Drinking a beer when someone is 20 or selling a sexual act don't result in injury to anyone other than the person that engages in them. 
	Some say they aren't victimless since they foster harmful attitudes in society. 

**Hate Crimes**
	Attacks based on a person's race, religion, or other characteristics. 


##### Crime Statistics
**Self Report Study**
	Collection of data gathered using voluntary response methods, such as questionnaires or telephone interviews.

##### US Criminal Justice System
**Criminal Justice System**
	Organization that exists to enforce a legal code. 

**Police**
	Civil force in charge of enforcing laws and public order at a federal, state, or community level. 
	There aren't any national police, but there are federal law enforcers. Like the FBI, ATF, DHS. 
	State police can enforce state laws. 
	Local / county police have limited jurisdiction to the town or county they serve in. 

**Courts**
	A court is a system that has the authority to make decisions based on law. 
	**Federal courts**
		Deal with federal matters. 
		Federal judges are selected by the president with the consent of Congress. 
	**State courts** 
		Vary in their structure but generally have 3 levels: trial courts, appellate courts, and state supreme courts. 
		**Trial Courts**
			Criminal and noncriminal cases go here, but noncriminal cases usually don't have a jury. 
			The jury decides who's guilty/liable.
			Unless a defendant is found not guilty, any member of the prosecution or defense (whichever is the losing side) can appeal the case to a higher court. 
		**Appellate Courts**
			Sometimes handle criminal cases that are appealed. 
		**State Supreme Courts**
			Highest state court

**Corrections**
	**Corrections System**
		Charged with supervising individuals who have been arrested, convicted, and sentenced for a criminal offense, plus anyone who's temporarily detained while awaiting hearings, trials, or other procedures. 
	**Prison**
		Facilities built for individuals serving sentences of more than a year. 
	**Jail**
		Provides temporary confinement, usually while an individual awaits trial or parole. 
	**Probation / Parole**
		Supervised by correctional officers after a period of incarceration in prison. 
		They can't associate with any known criminals or suspects.
