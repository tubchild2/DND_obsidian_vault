## Types of Societies
**Society**
	A group of people in a definable community that share the same culture components
	Many societies share a political authority

###### Preindustrial Societies
**Overview**
	Societies were smaller, rural, and dependent largely on local resources
	Economic production was limited to the amount of labor a human could provide. 
	There were very few specialized occupations. 

**Hunter-Gatherer Societies**
	Based around kinship or tribes
	Relied on their surroundings
	When resources were scarce, they moved, making them nomadic
	Common until several hundred years ago
	Aborigines tribes are modern day examples

**Pastoral Societies**
	Relied on the domestication of animals where circumstances permitted. 
	Able to breed livestock for food, clothing, and transportation, allowing for a surplus of goods. 
	Remained nomadic since they followed their animals to fresh feeding grounds. 
	Specialized occupations have begun to develop. 

**Horticultural Societies**
	Based on the capacity to grow and cultivate plants
	Formed in areas where rainfall and other conditions allowed them to grow stable crops. 
	Largely depended on their environment for survival, but they were able to build permanent settlements. 
	First revolution in human survival. 

**Agricultural Societies**
	Relied on permanent tools for survival. 
	New tools for digging and harvesting were made of metal. Towns and cities began to form. 
	People had the time and comfort to engage in more contemplative and thoughtful activities, such as music, poetry, and philosophy. 
	The Dawn of Civilization
	Social classes began to form and become more divisive. 

**Feudal Societies**
	Contained a strict hierarchical system of power based around land ownership and protection. 
	The nobility, known as lords, placed vassals in charge of pieces of land. In return for the resources the land provided, vassals promised to fight for their lords. 
	These individual pieces of land, known as fiefdoms, were cultivated by the lower class. Peasants were guaranteed a place to live and protection from outside enemies. 

###### Industrial Societies
**The Industrial Revolution**
	18th century
	Things that required months of labor now took days
	James Watt and Matthew Boulton created the steam engine
	People could get paper, glass, and could go out at night
	Maintaining family land and traditions mattered less than acquiring wealth and achieving upward mobility
	Labor unions formed to protect workers rights

###### Postindustrial Societies
**Information Societies / Digital Societies**
	Late 19th century
	Based on the production of information and services
	Digital technology is the steam engine of information societies
	Power lies with those in charge of storing and distributing information





## Types of Societies
###### Emile Durkheim and Functionalism
Durkheim stressed the necessary interconnectivity of all of society's elements. To him, society was greater than the sum of its parts. He likened society to a living organism. Even crime is important to society. 

**Collective Conscience**
	What Durkheim called the communal beliefs, morals, and attitudes of a society

**Social Integration**
	The strength of ties that people have to their social groups. Durkheim believed it was a key factor in social life. 

**Mechanical Solidarity**
	A type of social order maintained by the collective conscience of a culture. 
	Citizens act in a mechanical fashion: things are done mostly because they've always been done that way. 

**Organic Solidarity**
	A social order based around an acceptance of economic and social differences. 
	In capitalist societies, division of labor becomes so specialized that everyone is doing different things. 
	To Durkheim, once a society reaches this point, it has finished development. 

**Anomie**
	Literally means "without law." A situation in which society no longer has the support of a firm collective consciousness. Collective norms are weakened, and people are alienated from each other. 
	Experienced in times of social uncertainty, such as war or a great upturn or downturn in the economy. 



###### Karl Marx and Conflict Theory
**Base and Superstructure**
	To Marx, a society's economic character formed its base, and then the superstructure of culture and social institutions rested upon that base.
	To Marx, economy determined what a society would be like. 

**The Bourgeoisie**
	The owners of the means of production

**The Proletariat**
	The laborers

**Class Antagonisms**
	Social revolutions and conflicts that were the product of one class dominating another

**Bourgeoisie Revolution**
	Basically just capitalism and the early industrial revolutions horrible conditions for the proles. 

**Alienation**
	The condition in which the individual is isolated and divorced from his or her society, work, or sense of self. It essentially means that an individual has no control over his life. There are four types
		1. Alienation from the product of one's labor. An industrial worker doesn't have the opportunity to relate to the product he labors on. The worker doesn't care if he's making watches or cars, simply that the job exists. A worker may not even know or care what product to which he's contributing. 
		2. Alienation from the process of one's labor. A worker doesn't control the conditions of her job because she doesn't own the means of production. There's no room for creativity or change. 
		3. Alienation from others. Workers compete rather than cooperate. 
		4. Alienation from one's self. A final outcome of industrialization is a loss of connectivity between a worker and her occupation. Because there's nothing that ties a worker to her labor, there is no longer a sense of self. 

**False Consciousness**
	A condition in which the beliefs, ideals, or ideology of a person are not in the person's own best interest. Instead, it's the interests of the dominant class that are being imposed upon the proletariat. Ideas such as the emphasis of competition over cooperation clearly benefit the owners of industry. 

**Class Consciousness**
	Marx proposed that, to overcome false consciousness, that it must be replaced with class consciousness.
	It's the awareness of one's rank in society. Instead of just being an inert strata of society, the class could become an advocate for social improvements.  



###### Max Weber and Symbolic Interactionism
Weber's primary focus on the structure of society lay in the elements of class, status, and power. Weber saw class as economically determined. He also believed society was split between owners and laborers. Status on the other hand was based on noneconomic factors such as education, kinship, and religion. Both status and class determined an individual's power, or influence over ideas.

He was more concerned with how individuals experienced societal divisions than the divisions themselves. 

**Rationalization**
	A rational society is one built around logic and efficiency rather than morality or tradition. To Weber, capitalism is entirely rational.  Although this leads to efficiency and merit-based success, it can have negative effects when taken to the extreme. This is seen when rigid routines and strict design lead to a mechanized work environment and a focus on producing identical products in every location. 

**The Iron Cage**
	The culmination of industrialization, rationalization, and the like. In the Iron Cage, individuals are trapped by institutions and bureaucracy. This leads to a sense of disenchantment of the world. 





## Social Constructions of Reality
**The Social Construction of Reality**
	Written by Peter Berger and Thomas Luckmann
	They argued that society is created by humans and human interaction

**Habitualization**
	The argument that society is created by humans and human interaction
	Describes how any action that is repeated frequently becomes cast into a pattern
	Society is, in fact, "habit".

**Institutionalization**
	The act of implanting a convention or norm into society.
	A school is a school by consensus. 

**Thomas Theorem**
	If men define situations as real, they are real in consequences. 
	In other words, people's behavior can be determined by their subjective construction of reality rather than by objective reality. 
	If someone is repeatedly given a label, they might live up to the term even though it initially wasn't a part of his character. 

**Self-Fulfilling Prophecy**
	Our moral codes and social norms are created by "successive definitions of the situation."
	Robert K. Merton explained that even a false idea can become true if it's acted upon. 
	Reality is constructed around the idea
	People think the bank will run out of money --> they take their money out --> bank runs out of money



###### Roles and Status
**Roles**
	Patterns of behavior that we recognize in each other that are representative of a person's social status. 
	Roles exist like "brother," "student," and "neighbor."

**Status**
	Describes the responsibilities and benefits that a person experiences according to their rank and role in society. Refers to your rank in a social hierarchy.
	**Ascribed Status**
		Those you didn't select. Such as son. 
	**Achieved Status**
		Those you obtained by choice, such as High School Dropout, or nurse. 

**Role Sets**
	An array of roles. Statuses have complex role sets attached to them. 

**Role Strain**
	If too much is required of a single role, individuals can experience role strain. 

**Role Conflict**
	When one or more roles are contradictory. A parent who also has a full-time career can experience role conflict on a daily basis. 



###### Presentation of Self
**Role Performance**
	How a person expresses his or her role. 

**Dramaturgy**
	Erving Goffman presented the idea that a person is like an actor on a stage. Calling his theory dramaturgy, Goffman believed that we use "impression management" to present ourselves to others as we hope to be perceived. 
	Each situation is a new scene, and individuals perform different roles depending on who is present. 
	Even if you aren't consciously trying to alter your personality, different people probably see different sides of you. 
	In any scene there is an agreed shared reality between the players. 

**Impression Management**
	Think of a judge. They have multiple props to create an impression of fairness, gravity, and control--like their robe and gavel. Those entering the courtroom are expected to adhere to the scene being set. 
	Just imagine the "impression" that can be made by how a person dresses.

**Charles Cooley and the Looking Glass Self**
	According to Cooley, we base our image on what we think other people see. We imagine how we must appear to others, then react to this speculation. We don certain clothes, prepare our hair in a particular manner, wear makeup, use cologne, and the like--all with the notion that our presentation of ourselves is going to affect how others perceive us. 
	Our sense of self is based upon the idea that we imagine how we look to others, draw conclusions based upon their reactions to us, and then we develop our personal sense of self. 
	You are who you think they think you are
