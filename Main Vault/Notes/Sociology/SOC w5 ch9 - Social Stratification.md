
## Social Stratification
**Social Stratification**
	A society's categorization of its people into rankings based on factors like wealth, income, education, family background, and power. 
	You can be stratified by other things too, like race, gender, age.

**Socioeconomic Status (SES)**
	An individual's place within social stratification. 
	Society views people with more resources as the top layer. 
	Other groups represent the lower layers. 

**Counterargument to the American Dream**
	The American Dream argues that social standing is a product of your work and talent
	Sociologists recognize that the structure of society affects a person's social standing and therefore is created and supported by society. 

**Factors of Stratification**
	In most societies, it's **wealth** (net worth) and **income** (wages / investment dividends) that define stratification. 
	Other factors like age can be either be a good or a bad thing for stratification depending on the culture. 
	Parents typically pass their social position onto their children. 
	A society's occupational structure also determines social stratification. 

**System of Stratification**
	The method in which stratification occurs in a given society or culture
	How they operate reflects the values and beliefs of the given society or culture. 

**Closed Systems of Stratification**
	Accommodate little change in social position. 
	People cannot shift levels or have social relationships across levels. 
	Examples are estate, slavery, and caste systems. 

**Open Systems of Stratification**
	Based on achievement and allow for movement and interaction between layers and classes. 

**Ideology**
	Every culture has an ideology to support its system of stratification

**Exogamous Marriage**
	A union of spouses from different social categories. 
	Focuses on values such as love and compatibility. 

**Endogamous Marriage**
	A union of spouses from similar social categories. 
	Social conformities still exist that encourage people to pursue endogamous marriage


###### Stratification Systems
**Caste Systems**
	Closed stratification systems where people can do little or nothing to change the social standing of their birth. Talents, interests, and potential do not provide any opportunities. 
	Determines all aspects of an individual's life: occupations, marriage partners, and housing. 
	Promotes belief in fate, destiny, and the will of a higher power.
	**Hindu Caste System**
		Accepting their moral standing is considered a moral duty
		People are socialized to accept their social standing 
		Cultural values reinforce the system
		Been largely dismantled but is still deeply embedded in Indian society

**Class System**
	A **class** consists of a set of people who share similar status based on factors like wealth, income, education, family background, and occupation. 
	Class systems are open, since people can move to a different level than their parents. 

**Random System**
	I bet there's also a system in which people are assigned their roles in society at random. 

**Meritocracy**
	A hypothetical system in which social stratification is determined by personal effort and merit. 
	A meritocracy has never existed, but it'd be a fully open stratification system


###### Status Consistency
**Status Consistency**
	The consistency, or lack thereof, of an individual's rank across the factors that determine social stratification within a lifetime. 

**Primogeniture**
	A law stating that all property would be inherited by the firstborn son. 
	If the family had no son, it'd go to the next closest male relation. 
	Women couldn't inherit property. 


## Stratification in the US
Wealth is the most significant way of distinguishing classes in the USA

**Upper Class**
	Top 20% of earners
	Have power and control over their own lives, but their status gives them power over others' lives
	1% the US population, but own 33% of the country's wealth
	Old Money is inherited wealth
	New Money is wealth one earned and built themselves 

**Middle Class**
	Median 60% of earners
	$48,500 - $145,500 annually
	Have power and control over their own lives
	Broken into upper-middle and lower-middle
	Shrinking in the US in recent years
	**Upper Middle Class**
		Upper-middle class members tend to complete postgraduate degrees in business, management, law, or medicine
		Tend to pursue careers, own their homes, and travel on vacation. 
		Their children receive high-quality education and healthcare, and can have their special interests supported through tutoring, fostering more status consistency. 
	**Lower Middle Class**
		Lower-middle class members tend to complete a two-four year degree
		Hold jobs supervised by members of the upper-middle class. 
		People can afford a decent, mainstream lifestyle, but they struggle to maintain it. 
		They lack enough income to build savings. 
		Their grip on class status is more precarious. 

**Lower Class**
	Bottom 20% of earners
	Has little control over their work or lives.
	Less formal education and earn smaller incomes. 
	Often do routine work under close supervision.
	**Working Class**
		Often land steady jobs doing hands-on or physically demanding work
	**Working Poor**
		Unskilled, low-paying employment. 
		Their jobs rarely offer healthcare, or retirement planning, or are seasonal.
		Some lack a high school diploma.  
	**Underclass**
		A class that hasn't been questioned, and some believe its use is either overgeneralizing or incorrect
		They mainly live in inner cities, and are unemployed or underemployed. 
		They perform menial tasks for little pay. Some of them are homeless. 
		Many rely on welfare systems to provide food, medical care, and housing assistance. 
		More stress, poorer health, and suffer crises fairly regularly. 

###### Class Traits
**Class Traits**
	Also called class markers
	Typical behaviors, customs, and norms that define each class.
	Indicate the level of exposure a person has to a wide range of cultures. 
	Indicate the amount of resources a person has to spend on items like hobbies, vacations, and leisure activities. 
	They may define class traits, but are stereotypes
	Deviation can attract attention


###### Social Mobility
**Social Mobility**
	The ability of individuals to change positions within a social stratification system. 

**Upward Mobility**
	An increase when they move from a lower to a higher socioeconomic class

**Downward Mobility**
	Moving from a higher socioeconomical class to a lower one. 

**Intergenerational Mobility**
	When different generations of a family belong to varying social classes. 

**Intragenerational Mobility**
	Changes in a person's social mobility over the course of their lifetime. 

**Structural Mobility**
	Occurs when societal changes enable a whole group of people to move up or down the social class ladder. 


###### Standards of Living and Poverty
**Standard of Living**
	The level of wealth available to acquire the material necessities and comforts to maintain a specific lifestyle. 
	High standard of living reflects high quality of life.

**Feminization of Poverty**
	Acknowledges that women disproportionately make up the majority of individuals in poverty across the globe and have a lower standard of living. 
	Women make up 56% of Americans living in poverty. Potentially because of the wage gap and the struggle of single mothers to provide for their children. 

**Absolute Poverty**
	An economic condition in which a family or individual cannot afford the basic necessities, so day to day survival is in jeopardy. 

**Relative Poverty**
	An economic condition in which a family or individuals have 50% less income than the average median income. This is sometimes called the poverty line. 

**TANF**
	Temporary Assistance for Needy Families
	Provides cash assistance to families with children to help them attain economic self-sufficiency
	Time-limited, usually up to 5 years. 
	Adults must fall under an amount of annual income, usually half the poverty level, set by the state, to be eligible. 

**SNAP**
	Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
	Administered by the USDA and formerly known as the Food Stamp Program
	Began in the great depression when unmarketable or surplus food was distributed to the hungry. 
	During the height of the pandemic, participation reached 43 million people



## Global Stratification
**Global Stratification**
	Compares the wealth, status, power, and economic stability of countries around the world. 

**GNP**
	Gross National Product
	The value of goods and services produced by a nation's citizens both within its borders and abroad

**GDP**
	A country's national wealth. 
	Calculated annually. Totals the income of all people within its borders or the value of all goods and services produced in the country during the year. 
	Can be used to compare a country's economic health in relation to other countries

**First, Second, and Third World Countries**
	Outdated models.
	First and second world described industrialized nations
	Third world countries are "undeveloped."
	Lumps countries together that are quite different in terms of wealth, power, prestige, and economic stability. 

**PRB**
	Population Reference Bureau
	Takes on the job of comparing the wealth of nations. 
	Publishes an annual report that measures the relative economic well-being of all the world's countries using the GNI and PPP

**PPP**
	Purchasing Power Parity
	Economic theory and tool that compare the buying power of different national currencies by evaluating the cost of an identical item in both nations. 
	A measure of buying power

**GNI**
	Measures the current value of goods and services produced by a country
	A measure of productive output

**GNI PPP**
	A relative international unit that establishes the average relative income of a resident in that country
	Helps researches compare countries' standards of living



## Paradigm Perspectives
###### Functionalist
**Davis-Moore thesis**
- Posits that social stratification represents the inherently unequal value of different work
- Argues that the greater functional importance of a social role, the greater must be the reward. Certain tasks in society are more valuable than others
- The more skill a job requires, the more important it is. 
- He believed that rewarding more important work with higher levels of income, prestige, and power encourages people to work harder and longer

**Melvin Tumin**
- In 1953, Melvin Tumin argued that it doesn't explain inequalities in the education system or inequalities due to race or gender.
- Tumin believed social stratification prevented qualified people from attempting to fill roles. 


###### Conflict Theorist
Deeply critical of social stratification, asserting that it benefits only some people, not all of society. 
They believe it perpetuates inequality. 
Conflict theorists believe that the strained working relationship between employers and employees still exists, as it did when Mark critiqued industrialization. 


###### Symbolic Interactionist
Examines stratification from a micro-level perspective. 
Strives to explain how people's social standing affects their everyday interactions. 
Examines how people view themselves and how others interpret them based on class traits.

**Conspicuous Consumption**
	The purchase and use of certain products to make a social statement about status. 
	Carrying pricey but eco-friendly water bottles communicates a person's social standing, or what they'd like others to believe their social standing is.
