### The Anxiety of Kindness Study
I want to see how people would react to compliments and what they'd assume about the person giving the compliment.

1. Does race/gender/height/weight play a part in it?
2. How much would it impact people's mood?
3. What would people assume about the person giving the compliment?
4. What are the potential social ramifications of giving people's compliments?
5. Would people who receive compliments feel obligated to pass it on?
6. How often to people give and receive compliments?
7. Does being in public incentivize or disincentivize giving people compliments?
8. Is there a certain level of anxiety speaking to strangers?
9. Is there a certain level of anxiety complimenting the people you know?
10. Does the knowledge of being in a study change how people answer?


### Moral Roots
I want to explore the difference between morality and societal pressures. I could run an experiment that compares how people answer moral problems depending on their culture, the publicity of their answers, the rate of answering, and whether or not they're being analyzed. 

I hypothesize that some morals exist beyond societal forces and are indicative of human nature. Some norms are universal, and others aren't. Incest and patricide is universally viewed as wrong, while other things like sex and drinking vary more from culture to culture. 

I want to see how people answer moral questions on a scale from right to wrong depending on factors such as
- The cultures they identify with
- Whether or not they believe their responses will be publicly accessible
- Whether or not they believe their responses will be individually analyzed
- Whether or not someone else is in the room with them
- How much time they have to consider their responses


### Media vs. Agents of Socialization
Conduct a study that compares the impact media has on people's decision making compared to other agents of socialization. Ask them how they personally believe each agent ranks in importance to them, then present them with a number of open-ended questions and interviews. Then, after, prompt them again on how they felt each of the agents contributed to their decision making process.
