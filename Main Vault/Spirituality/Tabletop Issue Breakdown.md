**Overview of the Problem**
Me, and the kids in my hall are playing a dnd variant as Jehovah's Witnesses. The universe was created by a Ministerial Servant in the Aloha congregation and does not feature spiritism, demons, or the like. Magic is explained by a loosey-goosey particle called "[[Serendipita|ether]]" that isn't fully explained. 

While it does not bother my conscience, since I'm in full control of my actions and my part of his world, it does bother the consciences of several parents and elders. At a certain point, it is just DND but in a different, more witness-friendly, world. I would never hold it against someone if they were offended by it, and I respect their decision not to participate. 

The problem is that I don't want to be a cause for stumbling in the congregation, and I don't want my reputation as a potential regular pioneer to be harmed. 


**What is Magic?**
The definition of magic states that "magic is the use of special powers, secret rituals, or illusions to make impossible things happen." Tale Foundry is quoted as saying that "magic is the intentional suspension of physical laws through will, mystery, or the supernatural, contrasted with science, reproducible mechanics, and explained phenomena." 

That's wordy and ambiguous, but as a writer, magic is essentially a blanket term for things that violate natural laws. Because of this, "magic" comes in a lot of varieties and they fall on a spectrum from acceptable to offensive. The primary thing to keep in mind is that we want to avoid spiritism, divination, demonism, false religious teachings, and other things like these.

On the left of the spectrum, is something like the Force. It doesn't typically mesh with traditional magic aesthetically or culturally, and it has no spiritistic or demonic aspects. Additionally, the heavy scientific elements of the universe ground it and prevent it from being objectionable. 

Something further right than that is something like bending from Avatar: The Last Airbender. It resembles magic aesthetically, but bending in and of itself isn't spiritistic or demonic in nature. It's a biological trait you either have or don't, and the show is very clear in stating that. It does, however, feature a few iffy episodes, but overall the show is very grounded. It may require some defending, but it's mostly fine. 

Then we reach the line where most witnesses consider something morally wrong. I think something that rides that line is Lord of the Rings. It is the gold standard for magic aesthetically, and features debatably spiritistic and demonic ideas. Some are bothered by it, and some aren't. According to the lore, they probably aren't practicing any spiritism or demonism, but it's very ambiguous and confusing how it works unless you read the Silmarillion, which I don't want to do because it's extremely dense. Most of it boils down to "soft-magic," which is a magic system that isn't clearly defined by any rules or laws. I.E. "A Wizard did it!" is the basic explanation for how supernatural things exist. It's not really clearly right or wrong, which is why it's so debatable. 

All the way on the far right end of the spectrum is classic DND. It featured heavy spiritism, demonism, and a whole other slough of things witnesses should avoid. It is intrinsically and inseparably tied to these ideas in its base form. 


**Making DND Wholesome**
DND at its core is a roleplaying game where you make a character, and play as them in a world created by the GM. People enjoy playing it because of the freedom, the power fantasy, and the unmatched level of immersion. 

I was introduced to the game by Avery Gilmer, who created a DND universe that didn't have spiritism, demonism, or any of the other bad stuff. Mechanically, it was identical to DND, but the "magic" was explained away with a loosely defined particle called "[[Serendipita|Ether]]" that was bleeding into our world through glands that magic users had. They could use it and shape it into physical matter. Magical items were crafted with "[[Serendipita|Eathyst]]" which was crystallized [[Serendipita|Ether]]. Spells were called Spins, Magic users were called Funnelers, Wizards were Scholars, and so on.  

It wasn't a great explanation, and in my opinion, it falls right on the same line as Lord of the Rings. It's debatable whether it's offensive or not, since it's essentially rebranded DND. Essentially, it looks and feels like the same thing, but without any of the moral consequence. We use the exact same ruleset, it's just a different world free of any of the objectionable stuff. 

It worked well enough at first, and I got into the game because I like writing stories, making worlds, making rules for my magic systems, and sharing those with other people through an interactive medium. I created a campaign in Avery's universe called [[Arcohaem]], and I've spent hundreds of hours over the past year crafting that story to be as good as I can make it. I joined Avery's big campaign, and we've been in that one for about 5-6 sessions now. 


**When it Stopped Being Wholesome**
The Bible teaches not to be a stumbling block to others. When other people got offended, I (regrettably) became defensive. I argued that it didn't actually feature anything bad, and it didn't bother my conscience. That was the wrong attitude to have. The thing is, I don't *have* to stop doing anything because some other people disagree. That's fine, people have their own consciences, and I respect their decisions. I just don't want to have or foster that attitude in myself, or in the people I play with. 

I still do believe that in my part of Avery's world, there's nothing really morally wrong. My region is a grounded military story about propaganda and moral relativism. The real issue is the broader functionality of Avery's world being so loosely defined just to permit the mechanics of DND. I argue, if getting drunk was wrong, would you take a pill that simulated the same effects without being rooted in alcohol? I don't remember specifically where, but I remember studying a point that basically said not to seek loopholes in the truth. 

A very good friend of mine named Asher is also into DND. However, as I quickly learned upon joining one of his games, he plays fully loaded classic DND, including spiritism and demonism. Regrettably, I joined one of his campaigns. My character is just a really friendly idiot with a guitar, so I'm not doing anything bad, but some of the other players are playing Warlocks with patron deities. That is a bit too far for me.  


**Directions to Take**
So, here's the two major things. First, as a potential regular pioneer and influence on the younger ones in my hall, I don't want to offend people or encourage riding the line of acceptability in media. Second, Avery's world is far too similar to DND, to the point that I've caught Avery saying "spell" and "ritual" and other stuff like that several times. My region may not feature that, but his world and that style of DND isn't trying very hard to differentiate itself. Since it's the exact same ruleset, even some of my players say and do things like that. 

I know my brother has already decided to quit DND, and I'd like to as well. I want to completely divorce all of my games from it and create my own ruleset based on my own world that I know for a fact can't divulge into anything offensive. I want it to feature magic in the same sense as the force; it's supernatural, but it's the product of biology and the laws of that universe, rather than of spiritism. I want hard magic systems, not soft magic; I want no room for ambiguity. 

The issue lies in my connections to Avery, to Asher, and to my campaign.
1. If I quit DND, I first abandon Styxas. Styxas is Avery's campaign and the one that started it all. Is it very good? No, not at all. In fact, it's pretty terrible. I just don't want to hurt his feelings.
2. Second, I'd need to quit Asher's game, which is going to be extremely painful, since I really love that game and everyone in it. It'll ostracize me and make me look as if I'm judging them. 
3. Third, and by far the worst, I need to end [[Arcohaem]]. I've spent hundreds of hours crafting that story, and it's so deeply integrated into Avery's universe and to DND that I don't think they can be separated. We're only a third of the way through what is by far the greatest thing I've ever written, and it is genuinely heartbreaking to think of ending it this early. We just got out of act 1. 


**The Solution**
I am going to quit DND, which means quitting Avery and Asher's games. That much sucks but there's not much else I can do about it. I really don't want to, but I'll talk to them about it. 

The hard thing is going to be reworking [[Arcohaem]] to not be in [[Serendipita]]. 

In the new world, likely called Terra or something, [[Arcohaem]] is going to be an island somewhere akin to Norway geographically. There are other nations nearby, but they're separated by mountains or water. They have trade deals and such, but are isolated places. I'm literally going to take the entirety of [[Arcohaem]] and cut/paste it into a big ocean with new island neighbors.

Magic is going to be the product of mutation. There are common classes of mutations, and some really unique and creative ones. It's been this way since some biological catastrophe that occurred prior to the Draconic Era. Mutations are naturally dormant until your body and mind are pushed by extreme stress or trauma. Then, you can mutate and acquire your abilities. Mutations can be passed down to children. 

That same catastrophe probably created all the crazy species and monsters that exist in Terra too, chief among which were the Dragons. That kickstarted the Draconic Era. Some postulate they were involved in creating that disaster, but that doesn't make sense, does it?

Maybe I'll lift [[Sanguinine]] from my other universe and use it as the [[Serendipita|E-Tech]] replacement. Or, I can create an entirely new fictional power source (I'll probably do that since [[Sanguinine]] should be saved since it's SO FREAKING COOL). Regardless, some type of ore or crystal with latent power was discovered and began being mined. It caused rapid industrialization and the development of some crazy weapons and stuff in other parts of the world. [[Arcohaem]] resisted, and when it finally gave it, the Republic rushed into it, which is why it's so bulky and inefficient. 

[[Malfaust]] is just a crazy powerful dragon, and his tech is just very well designed with some really good materials. He's very intelligent. 


**The New Solution**
I'm quitting Styxas and ending Arcohaem A. I'll see how things go with Asher's game since his game is really silly so I doubt it's offensive. I'll keep Arcohaem in Avery's world since I'm not actually doing anything bad, I'll just limit references.
