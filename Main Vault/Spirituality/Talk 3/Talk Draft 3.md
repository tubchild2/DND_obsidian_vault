**Introduction**
How do you feel about what's happening in the news? Have you ever been asked that before? Chances are, you have. I mean, with how much is going on today, it feels like there's constantly something newer and crazier to talk about. And so, it's often surprising to people that we, as Jehovah's Witnesses, remain politically neutral. 

Because neutrality isn't common in the world, sometimes, people can confuse neutrality with indifference and maybe get a little frustrated. I'm sure a number of us have personally experienced something like this at work, or at school, or with family members. And it can be tough to be put on the spot like that. 

So, it's important to remind ourselves the reasons for our neutrality. Not only for ourselves, but also so we can explain our beliefs to others.

So, tonight we will be answering 3 questions related to our neutrality. First, what is neutrality. Second, why do we remain neutral? And third, how do we view governments? 



**What Does Neutrality Mean**
First, what is neutrality? Political neutrality is an active stance to remain impartial and refuse to take a side or show bias in political disputes, elections, or ideological conflicts. That's kind of wordy, but essentially it means that we don't lobby, we don't vote for political parties, we don't run for office, and we don't participate in political action to change governments. 

Neutrality doesn't mean that we're indifferent to people, or permissive of cruelty, or anti-government. In fact, as we'll see, a major reason we remain neutral is in imitation of Jesus, who deeply cared about people and actively encouraged people to *obey* the law. So quite the opposite actually. 



**Why We Remain Politically Neutral**
So, on that note, let's move on to question number two: why do we remain neutral? To put it simply, we're politically neutral in imitation of what the Bible teaches. 

As Christians, we strive to imitate the example of Jesus. Jesus wouldn't take political office, and he taught his followers to be "no part of this world." Or in other words, he taught that his disciples should remain completely separate from all political matters. So, we do the same today.

Another major reason we remain neutral is out of respect for God's Kingdom. We believe in its existence as a real government, and just as Jesus spoke of at Matthew 24:14, we share it with everyone we can. So, in a sense, we aren't politically neutral, we're just loyal to God's Kingdom. So, it'd be hypocritical of us to get involved with or put faith in any other governments.

In addition to obeying Bible commands, we receive many blessings because of our neutrality. For one, we have a completely unique sense of unity due to there being no political divisions among our people. Additionally, we can preach to just about anyone, just about anywhere, with very little risk of controversy. 

And Christian neutrality doesn't originate with Jehovah's Witnesses. We know pretty verifiably that the first-century Christians also practiced political neutrality. The book Beyond Good Intentions is quoted as saying, "though they believed they were obligated to honor the governing authorities, the early Christians did not believe in participating in political affairs." Multiple more secular sources support this as well. So, we're not interpreting it in our own way; this is what was intended for Christian living. 



**Our Attitude Towards Governments**
Finally, how do we view governments? To explain that, please turn with me to Romans 13:1, 2. That's 
Romans 13:1, 2. There, it states, 

"Let every person be in subjection to the superior authorities, for there is no authority except by God; the existing authorities stand placed in their relative positions by God." 

So what does this mean? 

This verse is encouraging Christians to submit to the authority of the governments under which they live as long as they do not violate God's laws. So, we obey the law, we pay our taxes, and we cooperate with the efforts of the government to provide for the welfare of its citizens. 

We also respect our position relative to others. We don't interfere with personal decisions through protests or by disrupting elections. We respect the rights of others to make decisions for themselves. 

So, in no way are we a threat to the communities we live in. Our peaceful beliefs and practices are so well known that the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine commented that Jehovah's Witnesses do not "undermine the security and integrity of any state." 




**Conclusion**
So, let's answer those three questions from the beginning: What is neutrality? Why are we neutral? And how do we view governments? 

Neutrality means not participating in politics, and remaining impartial in political affairs. 

We are neutral in imitation of Jesus' example, in obedience to scriptural counsel, and out of loyalty to God's kingdom. 

And we view governments as they are: superior authorities in their relative positions to God. Meaning we show respect to the governments we live under by obeying the law, paying our taxes, and cooperating with the authorities where needed, granted it doesn't violate God's standards. 

More than likely, you'll be asked to explain your stance on neutrality again. So, having a good understanding of our Christian neutrality will not only help us to remain neutral in the future, but it will help us to respond to others who have sincere questions quickly, and accurately, from the Bible.
