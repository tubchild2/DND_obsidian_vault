**Introduction**
How do you feel about what's happening in the news? Have you ever been asked that before? It's kind of a loaded question. 

Today, it feels like we are constantly being bombarded with news and events like never before. While it's not uncommon for people to discuss what's going on, with how much is happening on a daily basis, talking about politics can feel like a constant feature of our lives. 

With that being said, it's often surprising to our neighbors that Jehovah's Witnesses remain politically neutral. I'm sure a number of us have personally experienced being asked about something like this at work, school or with family members. Because neutrality isn't common in the world, sometimes, people can confuse neutrality with indifference and maybe get a little frustrated. 

So, it's important to remind ourselves the reasons for our neutrality. Not only for ourselves, but also so we can explain our beliefs to others. In this talk we will be answering 3 questions:

What is neutrality?
Why do we remain neutral?
And how does that change our view of governments? 



**What Does Neutrality Mean**
First, what is neutrality? It means we don't lobby, we don't vote for political parties, we won't run for office, and we also will not participate in political action to change governments. It does not mean that we're indifferent to people or anti-government. In fact, as we will see, a major part of the reason we remain neutral is in imitation of Jesus, who was incredibly loving and law abiding. So quite the opposite actually. 



**Why We Remain Politically Neutral**
So why do we remain neutral? To put it simply, we're politically neutral in imitation of what the Bible teaches. 

As witnesses, we strive to imitate the example of Jesus. Jesus wouldn't take political office, and he taught his followers to be "no part of this world." Or in other words, he taught that his disciples should remain completely separate from all political matters. So, we do the same today.

Jesus taught at Matthew 24:14 that the "...good news of the Kingdom would be preached in all the inhabited earth." As Jehovah's Witnesses we are loyal to God's Kingdom first, and a large part of our ministry revolves around preaching about it. In a sense, we aren't politically neutral, we're just loyal to God's Kingdom. So, it'd be hypocritical not to be neutral in the political affairs of any country.

There are advantages to this neutrality beyond that too. Because of our neutrality, we enjoy a completely unique sense of unity in our brotherhood. There are no political divisions among our people, no where we can't preach, and no one we can't preach to. We can remain completely unbiased and impartial to everyone.

And Christian neutrality doesn't originate with Jehovah's Witnesses. We know pretty verifiably that the first-century Christians also practiced political neutrality. The book Beyond Good Intentions is quoted as saying, "though they believed they were obligated to honor the governing authorities, the early Christians did not believe in participating in political affairs." Multiple more secular sources support this as well. 




**Our Attitude Towards Governments**
Finally, how does neutrality affect our view of governments? To explain that, please turn with me to 
Romans 13:1, 2. There, it states, 

"Let every person be in subjection to the superior authorities, for there is no authority except by God; the existing authorities stand placed in their relative positions by God." 

So what does this mean? In imitation of this scriptural counsel, by submitting to the authority of the governments under which we live. We obey the law, we pay our taxes, and we cooperate with the efforts of the government to provide for the welfare of its citizens. We will do as much as we can to be in subjection to the superior authorities, as long as they do not violate God's standards. 
- In imitation of this scriptural counsel, by submitting to the authority of the governments under which we live.

We also respect our position relative to others. We don't interfere with personal decisions through protests or by disrupting elections. We respect the rights of others to make decisions for themselves. 

So, in no way are we a threat to the communities we live in. Our peaceful beliefs and practices are so well known that the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine commented that Jehovah's Witnesses do not "undermine the security and integrity of any state." Certainly we are not a threat to national security.



**Conclusion**
So, let's answer those three questions from the beginning:

What is neutrality? -- Neutrality means not participating in politics, and remaining impartial in political affairs

Why are we neutral? -- We imitate Christ Jesus example, follow scriptural counsel, and out of loyalty to God's kingdom.

And how does that change our view of governments? --

We show respect to the governments we live under by obeying the law, paying our taxes, and cooperating with the authorities where needed, granted it doesn't violate God's standards. 

More than likely, the time may come when we will be asked to explain our stance on neutrality. So, having a good understanding of our Christian neutrality will not only help us to remain neutral in the future, but it will help us to respond to others who have sincere questions quickly, and accurately, from the Bible.
