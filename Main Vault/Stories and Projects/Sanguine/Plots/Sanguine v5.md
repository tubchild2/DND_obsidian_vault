### Overview
A comic series that centers around why you should be a good person in a world where cruelty is rewarded. Each issue follows Mell as he navigates a personal conflict as a hero, goes up against a villain, and engages in western-inspired superpowered action. 


### Chapters
###### Chapter 1: Origins
**Early Life**
Melvin Delaney (Mell) was born in 3151 to Martha and Hank Delaney. He is the younger brother of Jesse Delaney. 
Martha believed in protecting her own at all costs, and was a strong-willed and practical woman. She would ignore the wellbeing of others for those she loved. She had an extensive criminal record, including multiple crimes against the Red Culling. Hank is a funny and sweet man who was regularly taken advantage of by others. As a formerly religious man, Hank had a strict set of morals he never broke. 

When Mell was 9, and Jesse 12, they learned that they were both [[Ferromancy|Ferromancers]]. Mell's reckless use of his abilities in public alerted the Red Culling. To protect her kids, Martha killed and stole from some innocent people to hide Mell and Jesse. Before long, Inquisitor Primus visited their home. Martha tried to convince him that Mell and Jesse had passed on, but the Primus knew better. To punish her for crimes against the Culling, he stabbed her to death, and brutally wounded Hank. He then left, disappointed. 

Following their mother's death, Jesse took on a kill or be killed attitude about the world. If they didn't take extreme measures to ensure the safety of those around them, to a degree beyond what their mother had, they would die. He is objective, detached, and isn't above killing. He's greedy, but he has loving relationships that he does everything he can for. 

Mell blamed himself for his mother's death, and developed self-hate tendencies. He doesn't like himself, he thinks he's a bad person, and every mistake he makes feels monumental. He has supreme anxiety, depressive tendencies, and is prone to impulsivity. He's a good person at heart, who values his relationships over everything else. 


**Outlaw Days**
At the age of 16, Mell and Jesse joined the Jack Fives Gang and officially became outlaws. They kept this from their father. It was Jesse's idea, and Mell went along with it due to him being suggestible. 

When Mell was 18, Hank developed an undiagnosable condition that began slowly worsening over time. Maintaining his health becomes slowly more and more expensive over time. 


**Vigilantism**
Mell had just turned 20. Him and Jesse were high ranking members of the Jack Fives Gang, and were making good money. A disastrous accident with some rookies led to a lot of the gang's personal information getting out. This led to the execution of Mell's best friend Cliff. Mell realized how awful his lifestyle had become.

Mell wants to lead a responsible life, but he's wracked with guilt. He doesn't just want to be a good person, he wants to feel like a good person. He becomes a vigilante to go after bad people out of principle. He knows it's the right thing to do, and he knows he can. 

He has to keep his identity secret, since he's openly practicing [[Ferromancy]]. This is an incredibly risky thing to do, since it puts him at odds with the Red Culling. 

###### Chapter 2: Responsibility
As Mell begins his vigilantism, he has early doubts. He questions what his responsibilities actually are to the people of Terra, and whether what he's doing is right or not. 

Mell's brother begins pushing back against his new lifestyle. He argues that it isn't Mell's responsibility to be a hero to anyone, and that it's just going to ruin his life and get him killed. He appreciates Mell's loving care for people, but it's not realistic in this world. He debates Mell on what it means to be a "good" person. He mentions their mother, and what she believed about protecting her own. If she hadn't done what she had, they wouldn't be here. They failed to do that for her, and he won't fail to do that for Mell. 

The overwhelming presence and power of the Culling is enough to convince Mell that it's impossible to truly make a difference on a grand scale. 

Mell wants to quit, but it's when he saves his first person, a child that reminds him of Cliff, that he understands. He can't stop the Culling, but he can make a difference in people's lives. If he doesn't, no one else will.

Mell can't convince Jesse. Jesse says he still loves him, and will do whatever he can to protect him, but he can't respect or encourage that decision. They hug and say goodbye for now, as he fully quits the gang. 


###### Chapter 3: Balance
Mell meets a girl named Marion Miller, and the two get along very quickly. They both express interest in one another, and Mell feels fantastic. Unfortunately, pursuing her puts her at risk, and their relationship is always in conflict with his responsibilities. They begin to have troubles because of that.

He desires comfort, but his new moral lifestyle leaves him very little money, meaning he has very little very bad food, water, and shelter. A lot of his money is going to treating his father's illness. He's had to ask Jesse to cover more of it, which is an insane ask. Mell's part of the payments are still more than he can reasonably cover, even with the deductions. 

An anonymous outlaw begins hunting Mell. They wear a black and gold outfit and have extreme wealth. They specifically and fundamentally hate Mell and his worldview. They believe that it's kill or be killed, and wants to make Mell see that and quit because they're a lunatic. They're extremely successful because they take whatever they think they deserve, and Mell will always be jealous of that fact. 

Mell believes that in this world, being cruel is rewarded, and being good is punished. He wants to reward himself, and he feels entitled to it. This creates an inherent conflict between remaining a hero, and being happy in his personal life. 

Mell encounters a former bounty hunter and leader of a private police force. They were doing so much good, and they inspired Mell when he was younger. But, she wanted a family, so she quit. She abandoned all of her responsibilities to live a happy life. She says that everyone is entitled to some happiness, and it isn't selfish to want that. Mell disagrees, since her quitting led to hundreds of preventable deaths. 

Mell decides that his responsibilities matter more than what he wants. Mell has to break Marion's heart, and he can't tell her why. He then has to deal with the outlaw, who learns his identity and tries to kill his father and brother. He then has to confront his brother after all this. 


###### Chapter 4: Acceptance
His new workmate Austin Nicholl is getting frustrated with Mell. Mell's double-life accidentally hurts Austin on a deep level. Mell keeps trying to make it up to him, but it keeps getting worse. Austin holds grudges, and is convinced Mell is a bad person. He has a vindictive personality, so he starts being cruel to Mell in return. He wants to make Mell believe he's a bad person. 

Mell questions why anyone would like Austin in the first place, but as he gets to know him better, he learns to appreciate the flaws in the imperfect. There's beauty in it; it's Wabi-sabi. 

This helps Mell to better appreciate his own imperfections. 


###### Chapter 5: Vengeance
Mell has unchecked anger issues. He wants to get revenge on people, and it would make his life easier to just kill people who keep escaping justice. The number one person he wants to kill is Inquisitor Primus.
