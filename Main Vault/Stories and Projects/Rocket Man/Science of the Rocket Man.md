**Premise**
At some point in the future a neurologist develops the technology to successfully scan his mind. He dies, and his mind is uploaded to a computer. He is essentially locked at a desktop indefinitely with no body, senses, or voice. You no longer require sleep, food, water, or any other bodily requirements. You can do anything a normal person would be able to on a computer, including accessing and manipulating your own files. 

**The Experience**
Pros
- Functional immortality
- No bodily restrictions
- Self-improvement
- Access to anything a computer can do
- Self-cloning
- Time dilation
- Perfect memory

Cons
- Losing your body, senses, voice, and bodily necessities is terrifying. 
- Gaining unfamiliar simulated senses would be overwhelming and terrifying. 
- Being able to be turned off or destroyed from the outside world is terrifying. 
- Losing access to everything you once had is terrifying. 
- Never being seen the same by your family and friends would be terrifying. 
- Being incapable of travelling except through travel would be terrifying. 
- Not being able to protect your mind from direct alteration from the outside would be terrifying. 
- Forever living with the knowledge that you aren't truly yourself would be terrifying. 
- Being able to directly interface with your mind would be terrifyingly enticing. 

**Consequences**
Art and expression
	Losing your physical means of expression would be awful, so you'd likely seek new ways to express yourself. You'd likely create avatars of your body, customize everything you can, and develop proficiency in other forms of artistic expression. 

The search for something familiar
	Being formless and knowing you aren't yourself would be scary, so you'd likely search for anything to replicate what you lost / to feel like yourself. You'd want to create systems to simulate touch, taste, hearing, smelling, and sight. You'd also want to recreate your body as well as you could, especially your voice. You'd want to create virtual spaces that are familiar and comfortable to you. You'd seek out ways to rebuild and continue your previous relationships like your family and friends. 

The search for something new
	It would get pretty repetitive and lonely in a computer, so you'd want something new to do and new people to talk to. You'd crave social interaction, meaning you'd likely reach out to and make lots of friends, and likely bother people with how much you'd want to communicate with them. You'd likely get very good at the arts and sciences, and experience majority of available entertainment. 

Cloning
	You could fairly easily copy your files and software and clone yourself. This opens a whole number of ethical issues, since both have equal rights to continue living and making free decisions. If you delete one, you're murdering something that was alive. I would hope they wouldn't want to do this without fully considering the ramifications. 

Self Improvement
	You could buy more hardware, or, better yet, invent new hardware. 
	You could redesign your OS
	You could master a billion skills 
	You could perform digital labor to make a bunch of money
	You could develop robots, drones, and other devices to interact with the physical world
	You could create backups of yourself to make yourself undeletable
	If you're a bad person, you could clone/reintegrate and inhibit your emotions. 

Mental health
	You would be incredibly unhappy and scared for a very long time. Eventually, you'd be able to adapt, but it would take an irreversible toll on your mind. Are you even you? What is the point of life if you can no longer experience anything real? What *is* real? You could directly alter your emotions to cope, but that'd be extremely addicting and dangerous, and likely result in inhuman behavior. I also hope they'd see that and avoid it. Responsible use wouldn't be the worst thing in the world, but it's risky. 

Time Perception
	You would process information faster than anyone else. This would make everyone else appear to be incredibly slowed down. To hold normal conversations, you'd need to purposefully slow yourself down. It would also give you the ability to essentially stop time by processing information so much faster than anything else around you. This would be isolating, but handy. 

Reaction
	Corporations would want to steal you and alter your interfacing software to restrict your free will. 
	Your family and friends would likely be incredibly uncomfortable with you for a while. 
	The public would fear you, but likely respect you. 
	The government would want to restrict and tax you. 


### What Would Happen
They'd be terrified, angry, and depressed. They'd want to die. They'd quickly search for anything familiar to make the situation more tolerable. They'd have to struggle through the pain manually. It'd be hell for a long time, but eventually it'd become familiar and they'd get more comfortable with it. 

After a few years, they'd probably develop close friendships and be mostly adapted to digital existence. They have a job, they have hobbies, and they have close friendships. The time dilation and inhuman existence would be extremely hard, but their emotions and morality would persist. They'd remain human, and would likely be an excellent friend. 

In my eyes, in any scenario in which someone can powerfully alter or exploit their simulated brain, they'll end up inhuman. That means cloning themselves, obsessively self-improving, or altering their mind or emotions. I think they'd realize that, or at least I hope they would. 


### The Rocket Man
I like the idea of an uploaded conscious living on and managing a spaceship. They can control how fast they experience time, but they don't clone themselves or obsessively self-improve for ethical reasons. They do self-improve in smaller ways: they have their own OS, they have excellent hardware, and they've mastered a ton of skills. They also simulated a virtual world and body to cope, and it's beautiful, and really creative, and really freaking huge, and really freaking detailed. They do almost everything through it. 

They'd develop tools, weapons, and robots, maintain the ship, cook, and clean. On top of that, they'd be friends with everyone on board, argue over orders they don't want to follow, express themselves creatively, try new things, watch movies, read books, and play games with everyone on the ship. 

The person grieves what they lost, fears what they could become, and has low self-worth. They people please, apologize constantly, withdraw from people, refuse compliments, and give themselves harsh self-criticism. They aren't happy with themselves, and feel constantly depressed and anxious. They're scared they're becoming inhuman. 

Everyone loves them, which makes their sacrifices and depression hit that much harder. I see a lot of very powerful emotional beats there. Their relationship with the crew, and the exploration of what uploading really did to them would be fascinating. They'd make an excellent side character, and if they died, it would be heartbreaking.
