## OVERVIEW
We follow Mell Delaney, who becomes a vigilante. In this world, cruelty is encouraged and rewarded. His vigilantism makes his life hell, but is worth it in the end because he's helping and inspiring people. At its core, the story is about sacrifice.

It's a short series of novellas. Each one will begin with a short, character focused story. It shows Mell helping the little people and solving small crimes. It explores characters, elements of the worldbuilding, minor conflicts, and teases upcoming plot beats. The first novella will not feature one of these, but will instead be entirely dedicated to his origin story. The A plot will be a large-scale conflict that mirrors the B plot, which is a personal struggle Mell experiences as a consequence of heroism. Mell typically has to make increasingly difficult sacrifices to continue being a hero, but he's still determined to continue because of the good he's doing. 

The finale will fire every trigger set up throughout the series. All of the previous antagonists form a temporary alliance to kill Mell, every character conflict is pushed to its climax, and the plot is absolutely brutal to Mell. The antagonists disagree on methods and their fighting makes things even worse. To defeat them, he needs to let his father die, who has done absolutely nothing wrong to anyone and has spent the entire duration of the story suffering. All of the antagonists are captured and sent to jail, and he gets a rare shout of praise from the public. 


## CONFLICTS
###### Faith and Motivation
**Conflict**
	The world is too oppressive for him to realistically make a difference.
	Progress is regularly erased, and he needs to do the same thing over and over again. 
	Multiple of his wins are corrupted at the last minute and aren't satisfying. 
	He can't overcome the Red Culling. Killing ONE inquisitor is as monumental feat. 
	He isn't a [[Ferromancy|Ferromancer]], he's just an intelligent and experienced outlaw. He doesn't feel powerful enough. 
	He doesn't know if any of this is really his responsibility. 
	He has to keep getting back up anyways because it's right, and because no one else will.

**Inquisitor Primus**
	A nigh unstoppable member of the nigh unstoppable Red Culling. Wants to break Mell's spirit by demonstrating the futility of his actions. Him and his branch of the Culling corrupt Mell's victories, undo his progress, and oppress everyone and everything they can. His powers make him physically relentless. As a member of the Red Culling, he also hunts down young [[Ferromancy|Ferromancers]] and will kill them if they don't join him. He's obsessed with his work and is the Needle King's right hand man. His real name isn't known. 

**Clifford (Cliff) Foster**
	Cliff is a young [[Ferromancy|Ferromancer]] that was discovered by Inquisitor Primus. He refused to join, and so Primus killed his parents and wounded Cliff's left arm. Mell managed to intervene and get him out. 
	Mell starts to see Cliff as a son to him, and he becomes one of the only people he reveals his identity to. He cooks him food, teaches him how to conceal his identity, and they even learn more about how to use his powers. He passes down the lessons his father taught him, and helps him through his loss, and Cliff starts to see him as a father. 
	Primus' pursuit is relentless, and so Mell has to lose even more sleep to try to keep him alive. Mell becomes obsessive about trying to find a way to keep Cliff in his life and beat the Culling, but there's just no way to do it. It gets to a point where he can either send Cliff away, or he'll die. He has to let him go, and it breaks his heart. Cliff doesn't want to go, so he gets killed by Inquisitor Primus, and Mell blames himself. 

**Attorney Wendell Barnes**
	Prosecutes the people Mell saves and makes a ton of money doing it. Exploits the legal system like Saul Goodman, and actively helps the criminals he puts away get out, and the people he helps go in. 

**Dr. Elliot Cassidy**
	The world's leading expert on pre-impact technology. He's oppressed by the world around him, and can't complete his work. Him and Mell are both good hearted people who believe in being heroes. Additionally, they both believe the same fundamental truth: without power, you can't make a difference. His work allows him to discover the root cause for the [[Ferromancy|Ferromancer]]'s enhanced physical strength, and he's tempted to take it to become a hero. Mell's work finally inspires him to take it. The issue is that Dr. Cassidy doesn't have as many limitations as Mell, and wants to kill some big names that have wronged him. Power doesn't mean anything if you can't control it, and Dr. Cassidy definitely can't. Being powerful exposes more of who Dr. Cassidy really is, and Mell is tempted to take the same formula. He chooses not to because he learns you can make a difference without it, and because even if he can control it, he can't just force his beliefs onto people and expect them to follow. It takes time, and patience. 


###### Guilt and Pride
**Conflict**
	Guilt weighs heavily on his soul whenever he does anything for himself.
	Mell has to make horrible, impossible decisions that ruin lives. He can't save everyone.
	Some innocent people are harmed as a biproduct of his vigilantism. 
	Mell has self-hate tendencies, he thinks he's a terrible person, and every mistake feels monumental to him. 
	Mell has horrendous anxiety, depressive tendencies, and is prone to impulsive decision making. 
	He feels great about the good he's doing. 
	He helps kids, the elderly, the disabled, the depressed, and more. 
	He saves lives from gangs, outlaws, disasters, and more. 
	He puts an end to all of the really big and bad names in crime. 
	He inspires people to do more.

**Perry Mackland / The Debtor**
	Someone who was wronged by Mell. They don't just want to get revenge, they want to make Mell hate himself, and prove to him that he's a terrible person by any means necessary. They believe that his actions, while done with noble intentions, are doing more harm than good. They present moral quandaries to Mell and test him on his personal life, often in brutal ways. His real name is Perry Mackland. 

**Sister Maya (May) Ehlert**
	A nun and medic for a local church. Helps take care of the people Mell injures. Is disgusted by his violent actions, and encourages a more pacifistic approach based on her religion (which is a modified version of Catholicism). Wants to make him feel guilty. Mell will occasionally volunteer in his free time. 

**The Followers**
	A large group of people were inspired by Mell and his actions, but they just aren't him. He needs to stop them from getting themselves killed while risking their lives. Some of them have completely inaccurate, almost cult-like perceptions of him that lead them to doing some bad things in his name. He needs to stop them, and deal with the mixed impact he's having. There's some that try to do what he does, but are just risking their lives. 


###### Health and Comfort
**Conflict**
	His regular job is awful, doesn't pay much, and is in constant jeopardy. 
	He lacks a comfortable amount of money, food, water, and shelter. 
	He's exhausted from a lack of quality sleep (or sleep in general). 
	He is regularly beaten senseless and horrifically injured. His body is in bad shape. 
	His gear and medical bills are expensive. 
	He has pre-existing health conditions. 
	He wants to steal money and take shortcuts, but he can't. 

**Arthur Pierce**
	Pierce is a criminal mastermind that is the exaggerated version of what Mell could become. He was once a vigilante and bounty hunter that hunted down the scum of the earth, but then he wanted a safe family, so he quit and started a company. Hundreds died because he wasn't there to intervene, but was it really his responsibility? Was wanting to take care of himself and the people he loved really selfish? His company is now enormous, and it lords over the criminal underworld of Terra. He attacks Mell's entitlement and desire for self-care. He argues that Mell should take what he deserves and use his power to be happy. Is self-care selfish?

**Ruth Faro**
	Mell takes on bounty hunting work for the people he's going after anyways so that he gets paid for it. Faro promises Mell money, equipment, and constant work. However, she won't explain what she's doing or why, and will push Mell into doing bad things for money. She wants him to be obedient and selfish, like everyone else. She's manipulate him with threats about money. Quitting would be a death sentence unless Mell can find a better job, so this is a temporary compromise. 

**Philip Baskin**
	Mr. Baskin owns the building Mell lives in, as well as the building Martha and Hank live in. He is a greedy, selfish, and indifferent man. He'll evict them over missed payments and just wants Mell's money. Like Mr. Ditkovich. 


###### Love and Loneliness
**Conflict**
	He wants deep personal connections over everything else, and he can never have them. 
	His relationship with his girlfriend Marion is slowly and painfully choked out.
	His brutally wounded father Hank is dying and treatments are too expensive for him to cover anymore. 
	His brother Jesse resents him for not taking care of himself or his father. 
	His endangers the lives of any new friends he makes as a vigilante. 
	His alcoholic mother blames Mell for Hank's injuries and is abusive. 
	He feels isolated due to keeping secrets from everyone. 

**Hank Delaney**
	Hank was wounded by Inquisitor Primus and developed an unidentifiable terminal infection. Modern medicine can only slow what's coming. Hank and Mell have a very close relationship, so it's been very painful to watch. Hank has started lying to Mell about his condition to give him false hope, and has been hiding medical reports and his coughs. Mell knows what he's doing. He's a very loving old man who encourages Mell to continue doing what is right. 

**Martha Delaney**
	Martha is an alcoholic who's spending all her money on whiskey, and very little on Hank. She expects pity from her children because of her condition, so she expects them to cover all of Hank's expenses. She blames Mell for Hank's wounds because his mistakes as an outlaw are what attracted Inquisitor Primus to them in the first place. Hank took the fall for Mell and barely survived. Whenever Mell pushes back, Martha plays the victim. She resents him for not paying and being there for them, even though she isn't herself. Mell still loves her, and remembers the woman she used to be before all of this. He still covers her with a blanket when she passes out, and they still sometimes have breakfasts together. 

**Jesse Delaney**
	Jesse resents Mell for leaving his life as an outlaw. He's ruining his life, missing payments for Hank, and nearly dying in the process for something he can't talk about. He wants to take care of his own, and is frustrated with Mell keeping secrets from him. He won't attack Mell, but he will stoop to low places to try and make him confess and quit. When he learns the truth, he still disagrees, but is more understanding. His involvement and desire to protect Mell puts him in danger, and may get him killed. 

**Marion Miller**
	Marion is an innocent bystander. Her and Mell have a wholesome relationship, and there's nothing wrong with them wanting to pursue each other. Mell's secret double-life and her suspicions begin slowly choking their relationship. Mell has to break her heart, which is completely unfair to her. Marion is a sweet girl who stays in his life as a perpetual temptation, and she knows it. She'll flirt with him, ask him leading questions, and try to get him back. Eventually, she'll move on to someone else and won't shy away from rubbing it in, because Mell is the one who didn't act. 


###### Public Animosity and Wrath
**Conflict**
	Most people hate his hero persona for being counter to local law and the code of honor.  
	He's slandered and used as a scapegoat by the public. People don't want to be associated with him. 
	He's attacked and harassed by an onslaught of rivals, some of which personally target him and go too far. 
	Some people don't want to be saved by him. 
	Innocent people are endangered because of their relationship to him, forcing him to conceal his identity. 
	Those who know his identity go after his friends and family. 
	His morality prevents him from killing these people, which makes people hate him more. 
	Mell wants to kill people, and each mistake he makes pushes the public further away from him. 

**Jeffery Reeves / The Butcher**
	Reeves is a psychopathic mercenary. He doesn't like Mell and what he stands for. He views Mell as a weak-willed person that's too afraid to make any real change with his power. He attacks his morality by trying to get him to kill and take matters into his own hands. He tries to learn Mell's identity so he can go after his friends and family. He's a very methodical and intelligent killer. He works for the mayor on occasion, but also kills for the hell of it if he thinks people deserve it. He completely gets away with it because he's close to the mayor. He's beloved by the public for ending some very bad people and for being near to the mayor. He's a horrible human being with no one in his life. His brutality gets cleaner results and is adored by the public and threatens Mell's beliefs. 

**Mayor Beatrice Tackett**
	The Mayor of the town Mell protects slanders him and blames him for her own mistakes. She uses him as a scapegoat, portrays him as a monster, and exaggerates every mistake he makes. His indifference to local law and to the code of honor is insulting. She attacks his reputation, and tries to have him arrested and killed. Her policemen hunt Mell down and impede his work for no reason. 



## SMALL STORIES
**The Church**
Churches are symbols of god and of morality. A previously good character who's succumbed to evil returns to his church during a climactic battle with the hero and protagonist. A neat juxtaposition to show how far they've fallen. 

**Gold Rush**
A new cavern is discovered, and a bloody gold rush to get the [[Sanguinine]] begins. The territory is split between rival factions. The fighting escalates when outlaw gangs get involved. Much of the fighting takes place in the mines, which ultimately leads to a catastrophic mishandling of [[Sanguinine]]. 

**The Defector**
A previous member of the Red Culling faked his death to get away from them when the guilt wore too heavy on his soul. 

**Campfire Stories**
Inquisitors are presented as inhuman to those who haven't seen them. The Needle King himself is treated like the Devil; some doubt he's even real, but everyone is afraid of him. They speak of monsters, myths, and legends; tall tales of heroes and demons. 

**One Down**
A group attempt to kill an Inquisitor that's been harassing the members of their village. They use [[Sanguinine]] constructs and traps, but it still almost fails. 

**Salvagers**
Salvagers enter into a fresh set of underground ruins and make their way deep beneath the surface. They study pre-impact society, how it relates to [[Ferromancy]], and the impact itself. They reactivate some tech from the ruins and get to use the GUI. On the train ride home, they're attacked by pirates.

## EXTREMELY ROUGH DRAFT
Melvin Delaney is an outlaw with his brother Jesse. A job goes bad in Red Culling territory and Inquisitor Primus goes after Mell. His father Hank intervenes and takes the blame for Mell. He's brutally wounded by the Inquisitor, and develops a terminal infection. Martha starts drinking to cope, and slips back into alcoholism. 
Mell walks away from the outlaw life, blaming himself for the attack. He wants to do some good, so he attempts the legal route and encounters Wendell Barnes, who's protected Mayor Tackett. Wanting to do something philanthropical, he gets a job working with Dr. Cassidy, who's a pre-impact scientist. Dr. Cassidy wants to stick it to the big names, but isn't powerful enough to. 
A disaster happens in the area, and Melvin intervenes using some weapons from Dr. Cassidy's lab. It's risky as hell and he gets hurt, but he saves someone's life, and understands what he can do. He decides to become a vigilante. 
What sucks is that his work is immediately invalidated by the Mayor, who's taking the side of the criminals and muddying his victory. 

Without his income as an outlaw, he begins to accrue serious debt. His landlord, Mr. Baskin, threatens eviction. Hank is diagnosed with a terminal infection, and the bills to keep him alive are expensive. Martha's alcoholism is progressing, causing her to get fired from her job, making money even slimmer. Jesse is upset with Mell for quitting and not helping to take care of their parents. Hank is downplaying the situation for Mell, which is unpleasant for everyone. 
Dr. Cassidy isn't paying him enough, and their work is slow. Ruth Faro offers a way out: bounty hunting work. He's already going after some bad people, so at least he'll get paid. Ruth Faro encourages him to do things he doesn't agree with, or she'll cut his pay. 
His mission leads him to discovering a conspiracy tied to Arthur Pierce. He's being protected by Mayor Tackett and Mr. Barnes, but his company is tied to a plethora of criminal activity. Pierce tells him that he should quit and take care of himself. His lifestyle can't be healthy. He offers him a bribe, and Mell nearly takes it. He chooses to get him arrested instead. 
The arrest is ruined by Inquisitor Primus, who frees Pierce and replaces him with a puppet. Now, Pierce' company is after Mell, and the Mayor and the public are furious with him. 
Mell wants to steal and return to a life of crime, since income is becoming a problem. The situation with his family has been really hard on him, and he wants to spend more time with them than he can. 

Mell is following Inquisitor Primus, who (in collaboration with the mayor and Barnes) has been ruining his work and oppressing anything morally sound for years. He discovers that he's going after a kid named Clifford Foster, who was recently outed as a [[Ferromancy|Ferromancer]]. Primus kills his parents, and rams a rod through Cliff's arm. Mell manages to get Cliff out safely, but Primus is now hunting them both. 

Mell and Cliff begin to bond immediately. Mell helps him to cope with the loss of his parents, and teaches him how to conceal his identity and use his powers. Mell really cares for him, but Primus keeps appearing to try and kill him since he's a potential future threat to the Culling. Mell reveals his identity to Cliff, and Dr. Cassidy, begging him for help. 

Dr. Cassidy recently discovered the pre-impact serum used to activate the same abilities [[Ferromancy|Ferromancers]] have. Except, his serum would trigger *everything,* not just the traits the [[Sanguinine]] accidentally triggers. His serum is impure and risky as hell to take, but he wants to make a real difference and is fed up with the mistreatment he's getting from the Mayor. He's inspired by Mell's courage, and wonders if he could do it too. 

Primus' pursuit is so relentless and unavoidable and Mell and Cliff are forced to separate. Cliff returns, not wanting to leave Mell behind. Primus arrives at the lab due to a tip from the Mayor and tries to kill them all. Dr. Cassidy takes the impure serum and begins mutating. Him and Mell attempt to stop Primus, but it's not enough. Cliff dies, and Primus drags the body off. Dr. Cassidy is thoroughly brain damaged in the fight, and the mutation keeps going and getting worse over time. Humiliated, he runs off. 

Cassidy wants revenge on Primus, the mayor, and Barnes. He tries to kill the mayor, and Mell has to intervene and stop him. Cassidy is locked up, and the Mayor blames Mell. Mell wants to quit, since his work doesn't seem to be doing anything but making things worse. He wants money, and he wants to help him family. But he can't. 


Mell hasn't checked in on his parents in a while, since he was busy with Cliff. Hank is so happy to see him, but he's definitely getting worse. He's hiding how bad his condition is. Martha is furious; she resents Mell for not paying as much as Jesse, and blames him for the attack. She's drinking a lot more, and has a violent outburst. Jesse stops her from attacking Mell, and takes Mell outside to talk to him. He wants to learn what he's hiding, since he's really concerned about him. He presses him over and over about it, and gets to a point where he's genuinely furious with Mell for not telling him. 

Mell is feeling awful about himself since Cliff died and Dr. Cassidy went insane. Now that the lab was shut down, he's doing everything out of his apartment, which he has to keep from Mr. Baskin. Ruth Faro is dealing him some equipment, but it's not even close to the same quality as what he could build in Dr. Cassidy's lab. 

Multiple copycats now exist for Mell, since some people think what he's doing is awesome. Unfortunately, they aren't skilled enough to do anything. They're just putting their lives in danger, or doing things in Mell's name that he doesn't agree with. Mell intervenes with them, but it's too late. The mayor holds a rally to honor a "hero" named Jeffery Reeves. Reeves is a lunatic and a serial killer, but because of him killing some bad people, the the public adores him. A group of copycats suicide bomb the rally, nearly killing the mayor. They wound 22, and kill 12. Mell helps take the wounded to a church and triage center, where he meets Sister May. She guilts him about the damage he's causing. He gets a job helping there now that he lost his job with Dr. Cassidy.

Perry Mackland was horrifically scarred in the bombing. He already believed all of the mayor's propaganda about him, and was there during Dr. Cassidy's attack. He despises Mell, and wants to prove to him that what he's doing is wrong and that he isn't a good person. He starts doing some disgusting crimes to get Mell's attention, and then tries to prove that he's a selfish and terrible person with a very long and complex plan. Mell outsmarts him, and is forced to make a series of tough decisions. 

Mell is hated by the public, he has no money, his family is starting to hate him, his work doesn't seem to be working, and he feels horrible about himself. 


Mell meets a girl named Marion Miller at the triage center, and they hit it off immediately. She's just as empathetic and kind, and helps him to work through some of his issues. She goes with him to visit Hank, who's condition is worse than it has ever been. Martha is incoherent and unconscious, so Mell puts a blanket over her to help her sleep, and makes her breakfast in the morning. 

Jesse learns who Mell is, and is conflicted. He wants to protect him, so he begins going on missions with Mell like they used to. He still disagrees with Mell's actions, and wants him to stop. 

Mell's double life makes Marion suspicious, which begins to choke out their relationship. She is very interested in Mell, and keeps flirting with him. Unfortunately, Mell can't be in a relationship with her without endangering her life. 

The Mayor is sick of Mell, so she sends Jeffery Reeves after him, and begins a citywide manhunt. Jeffery is a freaking lunatic who follows him for a week to figure out who he is. By observing Jesse, he manages to figure it out. 

Jeffery attacks the Delaney home. He tortures Martha in front of Hank. Mell and Jesse arrive, and Jeffery tries to get him to kill by stabbing Martha. Mell and Jeffery begin to fight, but Jeffery is a much better fighter. Jesse arrives and tries to kill Jeffery upon seeing how wounded Mell is. Jeffery gets the upper hand fairly quickly and holds Jesse at gunpoint. Once again, he toys with Mell, trying to show how pointless sparing people is. Mell makes a move and Jeffery shoots Jesse through the chest. Mell tries to tend to Jesse's wounds, but Jeffery stops him, and tries to make him watch Jesse bleed out. Mell gets the upper hand and nearly kills him. Jeffery threatens to expose his identity if he doesn't kill him. Mell still chooses not to, and he gets Jeffery to go away by promising to quit. Mell tends to Jesse's wounds, but he's hospitalized. 

Mell quits, and Marion helps him at the hospital. He doesn't want to be hated, he doesn't want to be alone, he doesn't want to be injured constantly, he doesn't want to be broke, he doesn't want to abandon his family, he doesn't want to hate himself, and he doesn't want to keep feeling like what he's doing is for nothing. Mell and Marion begin dating.  


Mell starts a business and starts selling his gadgets, allowing him to pay off Mr. Baskin, and help to pay for both Hank and Jesse's medical bills. Jesse is recovering nicely and is back home, Hank isn't but he loves getting to see Mell so much more often. Mell and Marion are doing fantastic, and Mell feels happy. He loves the praise he's getting from his new coworkers, and from his customers (namely Faro). He's still volunteering with Sister May and Marion. 

But, a storm is brewing. Mayor Tackett, along with her lawyer and Reeves, manage to acquit a bunch of horrible people to help her cause. She wants to free her city from the Culling's control, so with the help of Perry Mackland, Arthur Pierce, Dr. Cassidy, and Jeffery Reeves, they attempt to start a territory war with the Red Culling. Inquisitor Primus and two other inquisitors are fighting them all off, and the city starts falling apart. The Mayor's policemen are waging war in the streets, Pierce took back control of his company and is funding Dr. Cassidy to make much worse weapons, Reeves is killing a bunch of innocent people in bombings to try and kill the Inquisitors, and Mackland initially rejected the offer, but started working with them to buy his freedom. 

Mell sees this and feels horribly guilty. If he goes and tries to stop them all, he'll more than likely die, and he'll have to give up his happy life. The church is bombed, and Mell can't take it anymore. He breaks up with Marion, speaks with Hank and Jesse, and then dons the mask. He uses his new businesses' laboratory to cook up his best gear yet, takes a bunch of equipment for himself, and then quits. Among other things, he crafted weapons to handle each of them individually. 

He manages to stop a huge conflict in the center of the city. His presence immediately invites challenge, and everyone, Primus, Mackland, Pierce, Cassidy, Reeves, and Tackett all now have reason to go after him. Their attacks are uncoordinated at first, and they get in each other's ways. Tackett reveals his identity to the public. Marion sees this, and understands what Mell was saying now. 

They enter the hospital to bait Mell with Hank. Jesse sees what's happening and hides. Mell arrives. Mackland threatens to poison Hank if he doesn't admit that he was wrong. Mell expresses that being a hero has been the worst few months of his life. He's had no money, no pride, no safety, no love, nothing. His life has completely fallen apart because of it. He's questioned if he can really even make a difference, and if the difference he's making was good in the first place. He questioned if he should leave a take care of himself. It was a terrible way to live, and he's needed to sacrifice everything for it. But it's worth it in the end, because no matter what he has to give up, he knows that several people wouldn't be alive if not for him, and he knows that he's making a difference in people's hearts, and inspiring them to try and do better. He says he's sorry to Mackland, and that he didn't mean to do anything to him. Mayor Tackett is a monster, and Mackland should know that by now. He pleads with Mackland not to poison Hank, and that he's just trying to do his best. Maybe things haven't gone so great at first, but if they worked together to try and make the world a better place, maybe they really could make a difference. Mackland puts down the vial for a moment and really thinks about what Mell said. Reeves calls Mackland a puss and shoots him and poisons Hank. Mell freaks out and they all start fighting. 

Mell arresting Pierce ruined his family life. Pierce wants him dead because of it, and his years of bounty hunting experience make him a pretty daunting threat. Mell disables his equipment with some acid. 

Dr. Cassidy is insane, but still coherent enough to be furious with Mell for betraying him. Cassidy says they could've rid the world of scum like Tackett themselves. Mell points out the obvious irony of them working together, but Cassidy is way too far gone to listen. Mell manages to inject him with a sedative. 

Jeffery Reeves baits Mell by killing innocent people. Mell furiously chases after Reeves, who ran off into Jesse's room. Mell nearly kills Reeves, but stops himself before he does. Reeves continues to point out how killing him earlier would've saved his father's life. 

As soon as Mell hears Primus coming, he locks the hell in. Mell tries everything to kill Primus, but nothing works. Mell is caught by Primus, and he admits he can't beat him, but he'll never stop trying. Jesse comes out of hiding and pushes Primus off the building and into the water. He sinks rapidly, and supposedly drowns.

Mell is shot again by the police, and then Tackett admits to being complicit in the murder of Martha. The policemen realize they might be fighting for the wrong cause. Tackett says that he can't imprison her, since the public will always hate him. He says he doesn't care what people think about him as long as he knows he's doing what's right. Mell pushes out of the crowd, and stumbles to Hank as he dies. He holds his hand, and he tells him that he can relax. He's going to take care of everything for him. He'll take care of the dog. Everything is ok. Hank dies. 

Tackett is removed as mayor, and replaced with someone else.
