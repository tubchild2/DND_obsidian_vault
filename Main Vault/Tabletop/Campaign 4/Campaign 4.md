##### Premise and Methodology
**Mission Statement:**
	Deliver an immersive and engaging plot that lets the players feel like they've become heroes. The players will start with absolutely nothing. They will acquire everything over the course of their adventure by *choice* and not by accident. 

**Methodology**
- Difficulty
	- Logarithmic Saw Difficulty Curve
	- Each arc, the difficulty the players face will begin easier than the last arc's climax, but end harder. 
	- Arcs will escalate in difficult less over time until they reach the climax. 
	- Early difficulty escalation will be great. 
- Mechanics
	- Custom TTRPG
	- DND 5.5e as basis
	- New classes, backgrounds, and species(?)
	- Turn / spotlight based combat
	- Future levels are secret to prevent longing for level ups
- Scope
	- Logarithmic Scope Escalation
	- Each arc, the scope the players operate at begins smaller than the last arc's climax, but ends larger.
	- Arcs will escalate in scope slightly less over time until they reach the climax. 
	- Early scope escalation will be great. 
	- Scope must match difficulty. 
- Power
	- Logarithmic Power Escalation
	- Progression starts rapid but slows over time
	- Loot and other upgrades won't exist at random, for free without consequence, or without challenge. 
	- Makes development feel earned while matching plot and difficulty pacing. 
	- Payoff
		- Players begin at level 0 and acquire their classes, loot, and upgrades themselves. Nothing is free, random, or without consequence/challenge.
		- Develop a rivalry with the villain, and make him hated. 
		- Create scenarios in which they can't help yet, or in which victory is unlikely
		- Create scenarios in which they get vengeance, save someone/something they care about, or progress personal goals. 
- Pacing
	- Saw Arc Pacing Escalation
	- Each arc, plot momentum begins lower than the previous arc's climax, but ends higher.
	- Allow for downtime and breathing room early in an arc
	- The players have a limited amount of time to accomplish their mission, but they have a lot of time to figure everything out. The clock prevents them from staying still for weeks at a time, but gives them enough freedom to relax. 
- Time and Arcs
	- Arcs begin 2-3 sessions each
	- Arcs increase by a session every couple arcs.
	- Lengthen the plot enough to allow for reasonable progression of difficulty, scope, and power. 
	- Let each arc carry one major narrative goal
	- Let each session contribute to the arc's narrative goal, and have a source of fun/intrigue.
- Agency
	- Players will have freedom to chart their own course through the world within limits to allow for guidance
	- Some plot points aren't tied to location to allow for deviation
	- Give each player a moment or major choice per arc
- Immersion
	- The players will travel greater and greater distances each arc
	- Encourage scenarios in which some players know things others don't
	- Worldbuilding
		- Engineered automation for speed, scope, detail, and accuracy
			- Simulate stages of society
			- Simulate family trees, relationships, and populations
		- World Bible
			- Concrete and immutable source
			- Referenced by everything else
			- Tracks all incoming data
		- Geography
			- Include landmarks
		- History
			- Hunter-gatherer >> pastoral >> horticultural >> agricultural >> feudal >? industrial >? information >? ???
		- Factions
			- Designed with sociological experience
		- NPCs
			- More identity and personality, especially important ones
	- Session 0
		- Campaign expectations
		- Necessary exposition
		- Collaborative team design
		- Playtesting


##### Narrative
**PLOT OVERVIEW**
The PCs progress from nothing and defeat a rising evil force through determination. The antagonist is the embodiment of gloom, cynicism, and fear, and (like Giygas) can only be overcome with hopes, prayers, and an actual miracle.

**THEMES**
The story is fundamentally about belief in oneself, but from a sociological perspective. This entire world is powered by the beliefs of many, and here beliefs become fact.
	1. How easily beliefs can be influences by agents of socialization, labels, propaganda, and context. 
	2. How beliefs are self-fulfilling prophecies, and affect our sense of self
	3. How much control we have over our beliefs

**PLOT**
- Inciting Incident
	- Meteorite begins an alien invasion
	- The primordial embodiment of gloom, cynicism, fear, ennui, and anomie, and the antithesis to individuality, fun, and whimsy is invading. He's a celestial named AOTH. 
	- He has multiple creatures he's sending out into the world. They're smart, strong, sneaky, and well coordinated. 
	- They disperse and begin causing problems to prepare the way for AOTH's arrival. 
	- Everyone is terrified because they don't want the world to become that way. They don't know what's going on, but they have hope someone will make things better...
	- A prophecy is formed.
- Our Heroes
	- LVL 0 dudes from a tiny village on the outskirts of society
	- They gain basic abilities, team up, and handle some small local threat.
	- They discover information on AOTH's arrival and the ticking clock begins
	- They develop a rivalry with AOTH's Avatar and are coined the prophesied heroes
- Trials and doubts
	- Sources
		- Family
		- Peers (subcultures, personal heroes, friends, celebrities)
		- Communities
		- Governments
		- Religions
		- Media (propaganda, books)
		- Oneself (comparison, Cooley, Mead, Goffman))
		- Labels
	- Reasons
		- Psychophobia
		- Ableism
		- Ageism
		- Racism (slavery, miscegenation, genocide)
		- Sexism
		- Classism (social stratification)
		- Role conflict
		- Role strain
		- Ignorance
		- Ideological Imperialism
	- They're relentlessly told they can't defeat AOTH
	- Some people appreciate them, but they're mistreated and challenged constantly
- Growth and determination
	- They encounter self fulfilling prophesies
	- They learn how beliefs affect our sense of self
	- They get a glimpse into the inner workings of the universe
	- They learn to take control over their beliefs
- Extradimensional battle with AOTH
	- They battle AOTH's avatar, who's the physical final boss
	- AOTH appears and he's an eldritch nightmare, and the emotional final boss
	- The people who they brought hope to throughout their journey assist in weakening AOTH
	- They defeat him through hopes, prayers, determination
	- AOTH disintegrates

**FOUNDATIONS**
[[ID_Shared Reality]]
All manner of flora and fauna and science and magic and religion can all coexist and are equally true. Each region has its own set of phenomena and unique traits. There are tons and tons of powerful artifacts, monsters, aliens, dimensions, and histories to find. If you can imagine it, it probably exists. It's a self-balancing system. 
1. The universe is a shared dream. The dream >> force of thought >> force of duality >> forces of good and evil >> forces of life and death >> the observers. Observers agreed upon reality based on the other forces. They became mankind. Primeval forces dispersed and swelled as they became more foundational to existence. 
2. Primeval or ancient ideas are almost impossible to overwrite and will enforce themselves back onto reality.
3. Newer ideas can be created and destroyed, because the anchors maintaining their belief can be altered or destroyed. 
4. Existence is built upon the dynamics of these ideas spawning from observers. Ideas 
5. A belief becomes universal the instant it is created, but it usually has small, initially local effects. A magic system that works in one place will work everywhere, unless the practitioners and objects anchoring it to reality are destroyed and it's forgotten. 
6. The more people believe something more intensely, the more true it is.
7. Multiple contradictory systems and understandings functionally coexist and are equally true. Attempts to disprove or analyze contradictory systems births new ideas for both and maintains their coexistence. 
8. Phenomena form the basis for one another's acceptance, meaning the world gets stranger over time. The universe won't trend towards realism because deviations are unpredictable and spiral quickly. 
9. The nature of the universe *is* illogical, and understanding it enough to exploit it requires insanity. 

**WORLDBUILDING ELEMENTS**
- **Aiden's Magical Compounds**. Additional elements exist in this universe that can form new chemical compounds. They cannot be mined, and can only be formed in supernatural places. They make up the walls of dimensional citadels, they form the teeth of some monsters, etc. They're also made of atoms but have different behavior due to a variety of unknown factors. Allows for exotic materials. 
- **Bioscientific Magic System**. Geography, terrain, temperatures, flora, and fauna in certain regions exist as extreme parodies of themselves. Plants can generate lightning, frogs can grow dynamite, and sand dunes can be a 100 stories tall. Any creature from all genres that you can imagine likely exist here. Allows for crazy monsters and aliens. 
- **Multiverse**. There are other universes, but they exist not because of changes, but because of communal belief they exist. This means each one serves a cosmic function or is really weird. Allows for exotic dimensions.
- There's a room between those two rooms. Spatial anomalies exist on occasion as a product of local superstition.
